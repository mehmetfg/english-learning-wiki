# The Man Who Burned Everything to Find One Truth

What if everything you believe is wrong? What if your memories are false? What if the world around you does not really exist? Most people hear these questions and move on. But one man, about four hundred years ago, decided to take them seriously. Very seriously.

His name was René Descartes. And what he did changed the way humans think about thinking.

---

## The Idea

Descartes was a French philosopher. A philosopher is a person who asks big questions about life, knowledge, and reality. He lived in the 1600s. At that time, many people accepted old ideas without question. The church said something. The old books said something. And people believed it.

Descartes was not happy with this. He wanted to find things that were truly, absolutely certain. Not probably true. Not almost true. Certain.

So he decided to do something unusual. He decided to doubt — to question and not believe — everything. Every single thing he had ever thought was true.

He called this method radical doubt. Radical means very extreme, going all the way.

---

## Deeper

He started with his senses — what he could see, hear, and touch. Can you trust your eyes? Not always. You can see things that are not there. You can dream and think it is real. You can be tricked by shadows and distances.

So he threw away his senses. He said: I cannot be certain about anything I see or feel.

Then he went further. Can he trust mathematics? Two plus two is four. That seems certain. But Descartes imagined something strange. He imagined a very powerful evil spirit — a demon — whose only job was to trick him. What if this demon made him believe two plus two is four, when it is actually not?

This sounds crazy. But Descartes was not trying to say the demon exists. He was testing his ideas. He was asking: is there anything I can know for certain, even if a demon is tricking me?

He kept going. He threw away science. He threw away history. He threw away everything he had ever learned.

And then he stopped. Because he found something he could not throw away.

---

## The Twist

Here is what Descartes realised. Even if a demon is tricking him, something still has to exist to be tricked. He is thinking right now. He is doubting right now. And you cannot think without existing. You cannot doubt without being real.

He cannot doubt that he is doubting. That is a contradiction — two ideas that cannot both be true at the same time.

So he wrote one of the most famous sentences in all of philosophy:

*"I think, therefore I am."*

In Latin, the original language he used, it is: *Cogito, ergo sum.*

Everything else might be false. The world might be an illusion — something that looks real but is not. But one thing is certain: he exists. Because he is thinking.

This was his one solid truth. His one piece of ground. And he decided to build everything else on top of it.

---

## Landing

You do not have to be a philosopher to find this useful. Descartes showed us something important. When everything feels uncertain, when you do not know what to believe, there is always a starting point. You are here. You are thinking. That is already something.

And there is another lesson. Descartes did not accept things just because other people said they were true. He questioned everything himself. That takes courage. Most of us prefer to believe what is comfortable. But sometimes the most interesting truths are hiding behind the questions nobody wants to ask.

What do you believe right now that you have never actually questioned?

## Words to Notice

**philosopher** — a person who thinks deeply about big questions: life, truth, knowledge, reality
*"The philosopher spent his life asking: what is happiness?"*

**doubt** — to feel unsure about something, to not fully believe it
*"She began to doubt her decision after reading more about it."*

**radical** — very extreme, going much further than usual
*"It was a radical idea — nobody had thought of it before."*

**illusion** — something that seems real or true, but is not
*"The mirror created an illusion of more space in the room."*

**contradiction** — two ideas that cannot both be true at the same time
*"He said he loves travelling but also hates leaving home. That is a contradiction."*
