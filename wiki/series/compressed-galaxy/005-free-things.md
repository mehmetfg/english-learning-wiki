# Why Free Things Are Never Actually Free

You open an app on your phone. It is free. No payment, no subscription. You use it every day. You think: great, this costs me nothing. But does it really?

An economist — someone who studies how money and resources work — would say no. Nothing is ever truly free. Every choice has a cost. Sometimes the cost is money. But sometimes the cost is something harder to see.

---

## The Idea

Let us start with a simple example. Imagine you have one free hour this evening. You can use it to watch a film, go for a walk, read a book, or call a friend. You choose to watch a film.

The film costs you nothing. But you did not do any of the other things. Those things had value for you. You gave them up. The most valuable thing you gave up — maybe calling your friend — is the real cost of watching that film.

Economists call this the opportunity cost. Opportunity means a chance to do something. The cost of any choice is the best alternative — the best other option — that you did not take.

This idea sounds simple. But it changes the way you see almost every decision.

---

## Deeper

Now go back to the free app. Instagram, for example, or any social media platform. You pay zero money. But you pay with your time. You pay with your attention. And attention is not free — it is one of the most valuable things you have.

And there is more. These apps are not charities. They do not give you a free service because they are kind. They give you a free service because your attention is worth money. They show you advertisements. They study your behaviour — how you use the app, what you click, what you stop to look at. They sell this information to other companies.

So the cost is not zero. The product is you.

There is a saying in the technology world: if you are not paying for the product, you are the product. This does not mean you should stop using free apps. It means you should understand the real exchange — what you are actually giving and getting.

---

## The Twist

Here is what makes opportunity cost really interesting. It does not only apply to money or time. It applies to every single resource you have — energy, focus, space in your life.

When you say yes to one thing, you automatically say no to other things. This is always true, but most of the time we do not see it. We only look at what we are choosing. We do not look at what we are giving up.

This is why very successful people — in business, in art, in sport — often say that learning to say no was the most important skill they developed. Not because they are negative or unfriendly. But because they understand that yes to one thing means no to everything else.

Every hour you spend doing something is an hour you do not spend doing something else. The question is not just "is this good?" The question is "is this the best thing I can do with this time?"

---

## Landing

The concept of opportunity cost is uncomfortable. It means that even good choices have costs. Even when you make the right decision, you lose something — the other good options you did not take.

But it is also freeing. Once you understand it, you stop feeling guilty about saying no. You understand that protecting your time and attention is not selfish — it is logical. Every yes has a price. The smart thing is to make sure the price is worth it.

So the next time something is free, pause for a moment. Ask yourself: what am I actually paying with? Time? Attention? Energy? Data? Privacy?

Free things are not always bad. But they are never actually free.

## Words to Notice

**economist** — a person who studies how money, resources, and markets work
*"Economists predicted the price of oil would rise."*

**opportunity cost** — the value of the best option you did not choose
*"The opportunity cost of going to university was four years of full salary."*

**alternative** — another option, a different choice you could make
*"If the train is full, the alternative is to take the bus."*

**resource** — something useful that you have, like time, money, or energy
*"Time is a limited resource — you cannot make more of it."*

**exchange** — giving one thing and receiving another thing in return
*"The exchange was simple: she worked, and they paid her."*
