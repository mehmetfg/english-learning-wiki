# Don Quixote
## Chapter 1: A New Life
### A2 Version Simple Language

---

> **Karşılaştırma notu:** Bu dosya [[Don-Quixote-Ch01-A-New-Life]] ile aynı hikayeyi anlatır.
> Fark şudur: Bu versiyon **saf A2** seviyesindedir. Cümleler çok kısadır. Kelimeler çok basittir.
> Diğer dosya **A2-B1** seviyesindedir. Cümleler biraz daha uzundur. Kelimeler biraz daha zengindir.
> İkisini okuyun. Hangisini daha kolay anladığınızı, hangisinde daha çok yeni şey öğrendiğinizi hissedin.

### Key Words

- **knight** a man in old times who wore metal clothes and fought bad people
- **armor** metal clothes that protect your body
- **squire** a helper who travels with a knight
- **adventure** an exciting trip with surprises
- **giant** a very, very big person in stories
- **inn** a place where you sleep and eat when you travel
- **lance** a long, sharp stick that a knight uses in a fight

---

### Part One: The Man

There was a man. His name was Alonso Quixano. He lived in a small village in Spain. The village was in a place called La Mancha. La Mancha is flat and dry. There are not many trees. The sun is very hot in summer.

Alonso was about fifty years old. He was thin. His face was long. He had a housekeeper. She cleaned his house and made his food. He also had a young niece. She helped in the house too. A young boy helped in the garden.

Alonso was not rich. But he was not poor. He had a small piece of land. He had a horse. He had a dog. He had some sheep.

He ate simple food every day. He had soup. He had bread. Sometimes he had meat. He spent his money on two things. He bought clothes. He bought books.

He loved books very much. But not all books. He loved books about knights. Knights were special men. They wore armor. They rode horses. They fought bad people. They helped poor people. They were brave and good.

Alonso read these books all day. He read in the morning. He read in the afternoon. He read at night. He sold his land to buy more books. He forgot about his sheep. He forgot about his house. He only thought about books.

He read so many books that something bad happened. He stopped sleeping well. He thought about knights all day and all night. Slowly, he could not tell the difference between a story and real life. He started to think that everything in his books was true.

He believed there were real dragons. He believed there were real giants. He believed there were real castles with magic in them. He believed all of it.

People said his brain was broken from too much reading. He was mad. But he did not know it. He felt very clear and very sure.

---

### Part Two: The Big Decision

One day, Alonso had a big idea. It was the most important idea of his life.

He would become a knight.

He would travel Spain on his horse. He would fight bad people. He would help the weak. He would correct things that were wrong. He would be like the knights in his books.

This idea made him very happy. He felt young. He felt strong. He felt that his life finally had a big purpose.

But first, he needed to get ready. A knight needs four things. He needs armor. He needs a good horse. He needs a name. He needs a lady to fight for. In all the books, a knight always fights for a beautiful lady.

Alonso started to work.

---

### Part Three: The Old Armor

In the back of his house, there was old armor. It was his great-grandfather's armor. It was dirty. It was rusty. It was broken in some places. Nobody had used it for a very long time.

But Alonso did not see old, broken armor. He saw something beautiful. He carried it out. He cleaned it. He polished it. He put it on.

There was a problem. The helmet had no visor. The visor is the metal part that protects your face. Without it, the helmet was not complete.

Alonso thought for a week. Then he had an idea. He made a visor from cardboard. He put it on the helmet. It looked good. He was happy.

He wanted to test it. He hit it with his sword. The cardboard broke immediately. Just one hit. It was gone.

He was sad for a moment. Then he made a new one. This time he used a little metal too. He attached it very carefully. He looked at it. It looked strong.

He decided not to test it this time. He thought: if I test it, it might break. Better to believe it is strong.

He put on the whole armor. He looked in a mirror. He looked serious. He looked like a knight. He was happy.

---

### Part Four: The Horse

He needed a horse. A knight needs a great horse. A fast, strong, beautiful horse.

He had a horse. But it was old. It was thin. Its legs were weak. It was slow. It was not a great horse. Most people would say it was a poor animal.

But Alonso did not see a poor animal. He saw a great horse. He decided to give it a new name. A knight's horse needs a special name. He thought for four days.

After four days, he chose a name: Rocinante.

In Spanish, rocín means a normal, cheap horse. But Rocinante means something more. It means "once a normal horse, but now something better." Alonso was very happy with this name. He thought it was clever.

---

### Part Five: His Name

He needed a name for himself too. He could not be just "Alonso Quixano." That was the name of a quiet, thin man from a small village. That was not a knight's name.

He thought carefully. He chose: Don Quixote.

Don is a word that means respect in Spanish. Quixote comes from the word for a part of armor on the leg. He thought this was a good, strong name.

But he also added his home. Great knights in the books always used their home in their name. So he became Don Quixote of La Mancha.

Don Quixote of La Mancha. He said it out loud. He liked it very much.

---

### Part Six: The Lady

One more thing. The most important thing. He needed a lady.

In every book about knights, there is a beautiful lady. The knight does everything for her. He fights for her. He suffers for her. She gives him a reason to be brave.

Don Quixote thought about this. He needed to find a lady to love.

He thought of a girl from a nearby village. Her name was Aldonza Lorenzo. She was a farm girl. She worked hard. She was healthy and strong. Don Quixote had seen her a few times in his life. He did not know her well. He had not really spoken to her.

But in his imagination, she was different. She was the most beautiful woman in the world. She was a princess. She was perfect.

He gave her a new name: Dulcinea del Toboso. Dulcinea sounds soft and sweet. Del Toboso means she is from the village of El Toboso. He was very happy with this name.

Dulcinea del Toboso. His lady. His reason to be brave.

Now he was ready.

---

### Part Seven: He Leaves

It was a hot morning in July. The sun was not up yet. Everyone in the village was still sleeping. Don Quixote put on his armor. He put the saddle on Rocinante. He took his lance and his shield.

He walked quietly out of the house. He did not tell his housekeeper. He did not tell his niece. He just left.

He led Rocinante out of the stable. The sky was dark blue. The stars were still out. The air was cool and quiet. He climbed onto the horse.

He began to ride.

He was very excited. His heart was beating fast. This was the moment he had waited for.

As he rode, he imagined a great book about him. He imagined a famous writer describing his first adventure. He spoke the words out loud as Rocinante walked slowly.

"The great knight Don Quixote of La Mancha left his home to travel the world and do great things."

He liked these words. He continued riding.

The sun came up. It became hot. The road was dusty. Rocinante walked slowly. Don Quixote thought about his adventures.

Then a problem came to his mind. He had not been made a proper knight. In the books, you must spend a night watching your armor. Then, in the morning, a lord must touch your shoulder with a sword. He must say the right words. Only then are you truly a knight.

Don Quixote had not done any of this. He just put on his armor and walked out.

This was a problem.

But he thought: the first important man I meet can make me a knight. That is the solution.

He felt better. He continued riding.

---

### Part Eight: The Inn

The sun was going down. Don Quixote and Rocinante were both tired. Don Quixote's legs hurt from the heavy armor.

Then he saw a building by the road. It was an inn. A large, plain building. Two women stood near the door.

But Don Quixote did not see an inn. He saw a beautiful castle. It had four towers. It had a bridge over water. It had flags. Everything was wonderful.

The two women were not just women. They were princesses standing at the castle gate.

A man with a horn walked past on the road. He was calling pigs home from the fields. It was a normal evening sound.

But Don Quixote heard it as a royal trumpet. Someone was announcing his arrival at the castle. He felt honored.

He rode to the door. The two women saw the strange man in old armor on a thin horse. They were scared. They turned to go inside.

"Do not run," said Don Quixote. He used very formal, old-fashioned words. "I am a knight. I do not hurt ladies."

The women stopped. They looked at him. They did not understand his old words. Then one of them started to laugh. The other one laughed too.

Don Quixote thought they were laughing because they were happy. He smiled.

---

### Part Nine: The Innkeeper

The innkeeper came out. He was a fat, kind man. He had seen many strange people in his inn. But he had never seen anyone like this.

He looked at Don Quixote. He saw the old armor. He saw the cardboard visor. He saw the thin old horse. He saw the serious face.

The innkeeper was a smart man. He could see this man was not dangerous. Just unusual. And this man could pay for a room. That was the important thing.

He walked forward and bowed.

Don Quixote looked down at him. In his imagination, the innkeeper was the great lord of the castle. A very important man.

"Sir," said Don Quixote, "I am a knight-errant. I travel the world looking for adventures. May I stay at your great castle tonight?"

The innkeeper looked at his inn. A castle? He looked around. No. Just an inn. But he was a smart man. He said nothing about this.

"Welcome," said the innkeeper. "Please come in. There is food and a stable for your horse."

Don Quixote was very pleased. He climbed down from Rocinante. It was not easy in his heavy armor. He almost fell. He walked to the door. But his lance was too long. He could not carry it through the door.

He leaned the lance against the wall. He went inside.

---

### Part Ten: Dinner at the Inn

The two women at the inn helped Don Quixote take off some of his armor. But the helmet was a problem. It had a green ribbon tied in a tight knot. They could not untie it. Don Quixote said they must not cut it.

So Don Quixote kept his helmet on. He sat at the table with his helmet on his head. He could not eat normally because of the visor. The women had to put food under the visor.

For wine, they used a hollow tube. They put one end under the visor. They poured the wine in slowly. Don Quixote drank this way without any complaint.

He thought everything was going very well. He did not see the women trying hard not to laugh.

---

### Part Eleven: The Big Request

After dinner, Don Quixote called the innkeeper. He had a very serious question.

"Tonight," said Don Quixote, "I want to watch over my armor all night. And in the morning, I ask you to make me a proper knight. To perform the ceremony."

The innkeeper looked at him. He understood now. This man was not completely sane. But he was a gentle man. He was not causing trouble. He believed everything he said.

The innkeeper thought quickly. He decided to play along.

"Of course," said the innkeeper. He spoke very formally. "I will do this. Watch your armor tonight in the yard. In the morning, I will make you a knight."

Don Quixote was very happy. He stood up and bowed many times. He thanked the innkeeper. He called him a noble lord.

The innkeeper added: "I was also a knight-errant, many years ago. So I know the ceremony perfectly."

This was a lie. The innkeeper had never been a knight. But Don Quixote believed every word. He looked at the innkeeper with great respect.

---

### Part Twelve: The Night Watch

That night, Don Quixote went to the yard. He put all his armor on a large water trough. A trough is a big box where animals drink water. He picked up his lance. He walked slowly back and forth in front of the trough. He was watching his armor.

The night was quiet. The moon was bright. Don Quixote walked and walked. He thought about Dulcinea. He said quiet words to himself. He was very happy.

After some hours, a man came to the yard. He needed to give water to his animals. The trough was covered with armor. He reached down to move the armor.

This was a mistake.

Don Quixote saw the man touch his armor. He stopped. His face became angry. He raised his lance very high.

"Stop!" he shouted. "Do not touch the armor of a great knight!"

The man did not listen. He kept moving the armor.

Don Quixote hit him on the head with the lance. The man fell down. He did not get up for a while.

Don Quixote went back to walking. He was calm again. He had protected his armor. He had done the right thing.

Later, a second man came. He also tried to move the armor. Don Quixote hit him too. This man fell and moved away quickly, shouting for help.

Now there was noise and confusion. People came running. Some men threw stones at Don Quixote from far away.

Don Quixote was very angry. He shouted at them. He told them this was very wrong. He told them a knight's armor was sacred.

The innkeeper came running. He could see the situation was becoming dangerous.

"Sir knight," said the innkeeper calmly. "You were very brave tonight. You have passed the test. You do not need to watch until morning. I will make you a knight right now."

Don Quixote stopped. He listened. He thought about this. It was unusual. But the innkeeper knew about these things.

He agreed.

---

### Part Thirteen: The Ceremony

The innkeeper got his book. It was actually his account book, where he wrote what guests owed him money. But in the dark yard, it looked like an important book.

One of the women held a small candle.

Don Quixote got down on one knee. He bowed his head. He was very still and very serious.

The innkeeper opened his book. He looked at the page. He was reading a list of prices for food. But he spoke in a deep, serious voice.

"By my authority as lord of this castle," said the innkeeper, "I make you a knight."

He closed the book. He hit Don Quixote on the back of the neck with his hand. Then he touched Don Quixote's right shoulder with a sword. Then his left shoulder.

"Rise, Sir Don Quixote of La Mancha," said the innkeeper.

Don Quixote stood up. He felt wonderful. He felt different. He felt like something important had just happened. He felt taller. He felt braver.

One woman put his sword around his waist. The other put on his spurs. They kept straight faces. It was very hard.

Don Quixote thanked everyone many times. He called them noble ladies. He thanked the innkeeper. He said he would never forget this castle.

The innkeeper told him gently: it is good to leave early in the morning. And a knight needs money. And clean shirts.

Don Quixote said he would think about it. Then he went to find Rocinante.

---

### Part Fourteen: The Road

Before sunrise, Don Quixote rode out of the inn yard. He was a knight now. A proper, official knight. He was very happy.

The road was empty. The sky was pale blue. The fields were quiet. He rode and thought about his future adventures.

But then he saw something coming toward him. It was a group of men. Six merchants from Toledo. They were going to buy silk. They had some servants with them.

Don Quixote stopped in the middle of the road. He held up his lance.

"Stop!" he cried. "Everyone must agree that there is no lady more beautiful than Dulcinea del Toboso!"

The merchants stopped. They looked at the man in the road. He was in old armor on a thin horse. He looked completely serious.

One merchant spoke. He was polite. "Sir knight," he said, "we do not know this lady. We have never seen her. We cannot say she is the most beautiful without any proof."

Don Quixote thought this was wrong. You must believe without proof. That is the rule.

He got very angry. He put his heels to Rocinante. He began to charge at the merchant.

But Rocinante stepped in a hole in the road. The horse fell forward. Don Quixote flew off the horse. He hit the ground hard. He could not get up. The armor was too heavy. He lay on his back like a turtle.

The merchants walked around him and continued down the road.

One servant was not a kind man. He picked up Don Quixote's lance. He broke it. He hit Don Quixote with the pieces. Many times.

Don Quixote shouted. He called for help. He said terrible things would happen to the servant. But no help came.

After a while, the servant stopped. The merchants left. Don Quixote was alone in the road.

---

### Part Fifteen: On the Ground

Don Quixote lay on the ground. He was in pain. But his spirit was strong.

He thought about the knights in his books. He remembered a story about a knight who was also left alone on the road. He said those words out loud.

"Where are you, my lady? Do you not know I am suffering?"

He lay there for some time. Then a farmer from his own village came down the road. This man knew Alonso Quixano. He saw the man lying on the road.

He came to help. He removed the visor from Don Quixote's face. He recognized his neighbor.

"Señor Quixano," said the farmer. "What happened to you?"

Don Quixote did not see his neighbor. He saw a character from a book. He called the farmer by a different name.

The farmer shook his head. He helped Don Quixote stand. He took off some of the armor. He put Don Quixote on his own horse. He put the broken armor on Rocinante's back. They walked home slowly.

Don Quixote talked the whole way. He talked about knights and adventures. The farmer listened. He could see his neighbor was not well. But he was a kind man. He listened and nodded.

---

### Part Sixteen: Home

It was dark when they got home. The farmer wanted people not to see them. He waited until most people were inside.

But the housekeeper and the niece were outside waiting. They had been worried all day. When they saw Don Quixote, they ran out.

"What happened?" said the housekeeper. "Where did you go? Why are you hurt?"

The niece said nothing. She took his arm and helped him inside.

They helped him to bed. They took off the armor. They brought food and water. They put a cool cloth on his head.

Don Quixote ate a little. Then he became quiet. His eyes were closing.

"Send for the priest and the barber," said the niece quietly. "We must decide what to do. He is completely mad."

"I know," said the housekeeper.

They watched him sleep. His face was calm. He was smiling a little. He was dreaming of adventures.

---

### Part Seventeen: The Books

While Don Quixote slept, the priest and the barber came. They looked at the books. The housekeeper was very angry.

"These books are the problem," she said. "He reads them all the time. They are full of stories about knights and monsters. Now he believes everything. He went out in old armor and he came back hurt and broken."

The priest looked at all the books. There were hundreds of them. Some were old. Some were new. Some were read many times.

"We must do something about these books," said the priest.

The housekeeper said: "We must burn them."

The priest wanted to look at each book first. He looked at them one by one. Some he saved. Some he said must be burned. The housekeeper carried books out. She threw them on a fire in the yard.

The books burned. The stories burned. The pages turned black.

The priest also sealed the library door. He covered the doorway with plaster. When Don Quixote woke up, he would not see the library. They would tell him an enchanter had taken it.

They thought Don Quixote would believe this easily. They were right.

---

### Part Eighteen: Three Weeks Later

For three weeks, Don Quixote stayed home. He was quiet. He was polite. He ate his meals. He talked with his friends. He seemed almost normal.

His family was happy. They thought maybe he was getting better.

But Don Quixote was not getting better. He was planning. He was thinking about a neighbor.

The neighbor's name was Sancho Panza. He was a farmer. He was short and round. He had a big stomach and small eyes. He was a simple but kind man. He was not rich. He hoped for something better.

One night, Don Quixote went to Sancho's house. He knocked on the door. Sancho came out.

Don Quixote told him everything. He talked about being a knight. He talked about adventures. He said he needed a squire, a helper to travel with him.

Sancho listened.

Then Don Quixote said the most important thing.

"In the books," said Don Quixote, "knights win kingdoms and islands in their adventures. They give the islands to their squires. If you come with me, Sancho, you could become the governor of an island."

Sancho's eyes went very wide.

A governor. Him, Sancho Panza, governor of an island. With a palace. With servants. With gold. His wife Teresa would be a great lady. His children would be noble.

"I will come," said Sancho.

He did not think very hard about whether this was possible. He wanted to believe it. So he believed it.

Don Quixote took his hand.

"We leave soon," said Don Quixote. "Tell nobody."

Sancho nodded. He went back inside. He dreamed about his island.

Don Quixote walked home in the dark. He was smiling. He had a squire. He was ready. This time, the adventures would be different.

A new life was beginning.

### After You Read

**A2 version what happened?**

- Alonso Quixano read too many books and became mad.
- He decided to become a knight called Don Quixote.
- He cleaned old armor and named his horse Rocinante.
- He invented a beautiful lady called Dulcinea del Toboso.
- He left home secretly in the morning.
- He went to an inn and thought it was a castle.
- An innkeeper performed a fake ceremony to make him a knight.
- He tried to fight some merchants on the road.
- His horse fell and he was beaten on the ground.
- A farmer took him home.
- His friends burned his books while he slept.
- He found a neighbor, Sancho Panza, and promised him an island.

### Level Comparison Guide

Read this, then read the A2-B1 version ([[Don-Quixote-Ch01-A-New-Life]]). Notice these differences:

| Feature | This version (A2) | Other version (A2-B1) |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence length | 5-10 words | 10-20 words |
| Vocabulary | Only very common words | Some less common words |
| Descriptions | Short and simple | Longer and more detailed |
| Emotions | Stated directly ("He was happy") | Shown through actions and thoughts |
| Reading speed | Fast, easy | Slower, more to think about |
| New words learned | Fewer | More |
| Enjoyment | Easier to follow | More satisfying to finish |

**Which is better?** Neither. They are different tools.
- Use A2 when you want to understand quickly.
- Use A2-B1 when you want to learn more and go deeper.
- The best learners read both, at different times.
