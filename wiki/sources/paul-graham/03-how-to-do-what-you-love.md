## The Essay Key Passages

### Part 1: The Commandment

To do something well you have to like it. That idea is not exactly novel. We've got it in one form or another from everyone from Confucius to Benjamin Franklin. The real question is not whether you should do what you love, but how.

If you're young and ambitious, the main obstacle is not that you don't know what to do. The main obstacle is that you don't know what you like. You haven't given yourself enough of a chance to find out.

*(Gerçek engel, ne yapacağını bilmemek değil. Asıl engel, neyi sevdiğini bilmemek. Kendinize bulmak için yeterince şans vermemişsinizdir.)*

---

### Part 2: What Love Means Here

To be happy I think you have to be doing something you not only enjoy, but admire. You have to be able to think, at the end of the day, "Well, at least I did that well." Whereas if you spend your day doing work you don't admire, you feel ashamed of or at best indifferent to your life.

I'm talking about the feeling of being in exactly the right place. That you'd be doing this even if you weren't paid. That it matters, and you're the right person to do it.

*(Tam doğru yerde olma hissi. Ödeme almasanız bile yapacağınız şey. Önemli olduğu ve bunu yapacak doğru kişi olduğunuz his.)*

The test is: would you do this even if you weren't paid for it?

---

### Part 3: The Prestige Trap

The thing is, what we actually like doing is often not what seems impressive to others. There's enormous social pressure to work on things that appear prestigious that impress your parents, your peers, your neighbours.

Prestige is the opinion of the rest of the world. When you make decisions based on prestige, you're optimizing for other people's opinions about your life. That's a slow way to die.

*(Prestij, dünyanın geri kalanının görüşüdür. Prestije göre karar verdiğinizde, hayatınız hakkında başkalarının görüşlerini optimize ediyorsunuz. Bu yavaş bir ölüm biçimidir.)*

The most dangerous form of prestige is the kind that convinces you it's your own preference. When you say "I want to be a lawyer" but you actually mean "being a lawyer is what impressive people do and I want to be impressive" that's prestige talking, not you.

---

### Part 4: What Parents Do

One reason people end up in careers they don't love is that they listened to their parents. I don't say this to blame parents. Parents want their children to be secure. Security means choosing a career that is traditionally valued medicine, law, finance.

But security and meaning are often in conflict. The things that are most secure the most reliably well-paid are often not the most interesting. And if you spend decades doing something uninteresting for the sake of security, you've made a very bad trade.

*(Güvenlik ve anlam çoğunlukla çelişir. En güvenli en güvenilir şekilde iyi ödeme alan şeyler genellikle en ilginç olanlar değildir.)*

Good parents want their kids to be happy, not just secure. But it's hard to say "follow your passion" when you don't know what that passion is.

---

### Part 5: The Test

The test for whether something is work or play is simple. Ask yourself: would you do this in your spare time?

The things you do in your spare time without being paid these are the clues. These are the signals. Most people ignore them because they seem too small, too personal, too embarrassing to build a career on.

But the internet has changed this. Almost anything you genuinely love can now be turned into a livelihood. The question is not whether it can be done, but whether you have the courage to try.

*(İnternetin değiştirdiği şey bu. Gerçekten sevdiğin neredeyse her şey artık bir geçim kaynağına dönüştürülebilir. Soru artık "yapılabilir mi?" değil "deneyecek cesaretin var mı?")*

---

### Part 6: Don't Decide Too Soon

One of the worst things you can do is to decide too soon what you want to be. Not because you'll always be wrong, but because you narrow your options before you have enough information.

The right approach is to keep your options open as long as possible. Try many things. Notice what you're drawn to. Notice what you do without being asked. Notice what you're good at without much effort.

*(Doğru yaklaşım seçeneklerinizi mümkün olduğunca uzun süre açık tutmaktır. Birçok şey deneyin. Neye çekildiğinizi fark edin. Sorulmadan ne yaptığınıza dikkat edin.)*

Don't optimize for the right answer optimize for more information about what the right answer might be.

---

### Part 7: Always Produce

Procrastination is the enemy. Almost everyone who ends up doing great work has one habit in common: they always produce something. Even when it's bad. Even when no one is watching. They are always making things.

If you want to do something, make something. The making is how you find out if you actually like it. Reading about it, talking about it, planning to do it these are all substitutes for the real thing.

*(Bir şey yapmak istiyorsan, bir şey yap. Yapmak, gerçekten sevip sevmediğini bulmanın yoludur.)*

The discipline of producing something regularly even if it's small, even if it's bad is how you find what you love. And it's how you get better at it.

---

### Part 8: The Quitting Trap

There's a common pattern among people who waste their lives. They have potential. They know they have potential. But they never do the work. They are constantly planning to work, occasionally starting work, but rarely finishing anything.

Talking about doing great work is not the same as doing great work. The world rewards people who make things, not people who think about making things.

*(Harika işler yapmayı konuşmak, harika işler yapmakla aynı şey değildir. Dünya bir şeyler yapan insanları ödüllendiriyor, bir şeyler yapmayı düşünen insanları değil.)*

---

### Part 9: The Full Answer

So what's the answer? Do what you love. But:
- Don't decide too soon
- Test by doing, not by planning
- Ignore prestige
- Ignore what your parents want (lovingly but firmly)
- Notice what you do when no one is watching
- Notice what feels like play to you but work to others
- Always produce something

*(Kendin için bir şey üretirken kimse seni izlemiyorken yaptıklarına dikkat et. Başkalarına iş gibi görünen ama sana oyun gibi gelen şeye dikkat et.)*

## Glossary

- **livelihood** the means of earning money to live | *geçim kaynağı*
- **prestige** widespread respect earned by impressive achievements or social position | *prestij, itibar*
- **to admire** to look at or think of with respect and approval | *hayran olmak, takdir etmek*
- **indifferent** having no particular interest or concern about something | *ilgisiz, umursamaz*
- **to procrastinate** to delay doing something, especially tasks | *ertelemek, ağırdan almak*
- **potential** the capacity to develop into something great | *potansiyel, kapasite*
- **spare time** free time; time when you are not working | *boş zaman*
- **to narrow (options)** to reduce the range of possibilities | *seçenekleri daraltmak*
- **discipline** *(here)* regular practice; doing something consistently | *disiplin, düzenli uygulama*
- **substitute** something used instead of something else | *ikame, yerine geçen şey*
- **courage** the ability to do something frightening; bravery | *cesaret*
- **to be drawn to** to feel naturally attracted or interested in | *çekilmek, ilgi duymak*
- **to convince** to cause someone to believe something | *ikna etmek*
- **clue** a piece of information that helps solve a problem | *ipucu*
- **to admire** to look at or think of with respect | *takdir etmek, hayranlık duymak*
