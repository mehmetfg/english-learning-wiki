# 01 Life Is Short

## Giriş

Bu essay Paul Graham'ın en çok okunan ve paylaşılan yazılarından biri. Ocak 2016'da, iki çocuğu olduktan sonra yazdı. Başlangıç sorusu şu: *"Life is short" diyoruz ama bu gerçekten ne anlama geliyor? Ve ne yapmalıyız?"*

## The Essay Tam Metin

### Part 1: The Question

Life is short, as everyone knows. When I was a kid I used to wonder about this. Is life actually short, or are we really complaining about its finiteness? Would any amount of time seem short once you had only a certain amount left? I eventually decided that yes, life actually is short. Not just in the "it seems short" sense, but objectively short shorter than it should be for the things we want to do.

Having kids showed me how to convert a continuous quantity, time, into discrete quantities. You only get 52 weekends with your 2-year-old. If Christmas-time equals happiness, then you have 10 of them before the kids go to school. If you can afford a summer vacation every year, you get only 18 of those with your kid before they go off on their own. These things that happen so naturally now will each happen at most a handful of times. Knowing that made me feel them more acutely.

It's not just that having children showed me the finiteness of time. It also showed me what to do with it. Having a young child is like living with someone who is constantly in crisis. The emergencies are almost always trivial but they feel like emergencies. This prepares you, by analogy, for your own life. If you're going to spend your life with someone who is always in crisis, you need to keep things simple so you can respond quickly to whatever happens. That's what you need to do with your own life too. Keep it simple.

---

### Part 2: Shortness as a Surprise

If life is short, we should expect its shortness to take us by surprise. And that is just what tends to happen. You take things for granted, and then they're gone. You think you can always write that book, or climb that mountain, or whatever, and then you realize the window has closed. The saddest windows close when other people die. Their lives are short too. After my mother died, I wished I'd spent more time with her. I didn't, because I thought she'd always be there. But of course she wouldn't.

*(Annem öldükten sonra, onunla daha fazla zaman geçirmiş olmayı diledim. Hep orada olacağını düşündüğüm için geçirmedim. Ama tabii ki olmayacaktı.)*

Don't wait for the "perfect moment." Moments don't get better by waiting. They get rarer.

---

### Part 3: The Problem of Junk

What should you do with your limited life? There are all kinds of things you might think you should do, but the first step is to eliminate the unnecessary ones. Prioritize ruthlessly.

One of the most striking things about the way people spend their lives is how much of it is spent on junk. What are the "junk food equivalents" of activities and relationships? Things that won't matter in the long run. Things done for the wrong reasons. Things you do not because they matter to you, but because you feel you should, or because you're trying to avoid the unpleasant feeling of not doing them.

I have a heuristic for this: what would I think of this from the future? Not from five years from now from the last day of your life, looking back. From that vantage point, a lot of things that feel urgent today look like junk.

*(Bunun için bir sezgisel yolum var: Bunu gelecekten nasıl değerlendiririm? Beş yıl sonrasından değil hayatının son gününden geriye bakarak. O açıdan bakıldığında, bugün acil görünen pek çok şey saçma sapan görünür.)*

You can think of your life as a resource a non-renewable resource that depletes every day. If you spend it on junk, you've used a non-renewable resource on something that doesn't compound, doesn't grow, doesn't leave anything behind.

---

### Part 4: Junk Relations

Junk is not just about activities. It's about people too. Some relationships are junk relationships. They don't make you better. They don't inspire you. They don't challenge you. They just consume your time without giving anything back. If a friend even an old friend has become a source of consistent negativity in your life, you don't need to be dramatic about it. You can just let the relationship fade.

Cultivate your best relationships. These are the people who, when you think of them, make you feel charged rather than drained. These are the people who you should make time for, even when life is busy. Especially when life is busy.

*(En iyi ilişkilerini besle. Bunlar, düşündüğünde seni tüketmek yerine şarj eden insanlar.)*

---

### Part 5: Saying No

One of the most important techniques for shortening your life is to say yes to things out of politeness or peer pressure, and then secretly resent them.

Saying no is saying yes to something else. Every "yes" to a meeting, a project, a relationship, or a commitment is also a "no" to something you'd rather be doing. The question is not "Can I say no?" The question is: "What am I saying no to by saying yes to this?"

Eliminating the unnecessary is not the same as doing nothing. You're freeing up time for the necessary. And the necessary turns out to be much simpler than we imagine.

---

### Part 6: The Positive Case What To Do

So what should you do with your life? The answer I've come to is: do what genuinely interests you, with people you genuinely like. Simple. But harder than it sounds.

The tricky part is that what genuinely interests you is not always what seems most impressive. There is enormous social pressure to work on things that seem impressive in the eyes of your parents, your peers, your society. This is the junk trap applied to ambitions.

The way to escape the junk trap is not to ask "what seems impressive?" but to ask "what do I actually want?" And that question is harder to answer than it seems, because our desires are partly constructed by what we think we're supposed to want.

*(Junk tuzağından çıkış yolu, "ne etkileyici görünüyor?" diye sormak değil, "gerçekten ne istiyorum?" diye sormaktır. Ve bu soru, göründüğünden daha zordur çünkü arzularımız kısmen ne istememiz gerektiğini düşündüğümüz şeyle inşa edilir.)*

Answering it takes honesty. It takes willingness to ignore external pressures. And it takes the willingness to be a little embarrassed because the things we genuinely want often seem small or uncool compared to what we're "supposed" to want.

---

### Part 7: Now

There is no future version of you who will have more time. The future version of you will have exactly the same problem: not enough time.

So start now. Not when you feel ready. Not when the moment is right. Now, with what you have.

If something keeps showing up in your mind as something you want to do, take that seriously. That persistent feeling is not noise. It's signal.

*(Yapmak istediğin bir şey zihninize sürekli dönüp geliyorsa, bunu ciddiye al. Bu ısrarcı his gürültü değil sinyaldir.)*

Stop deferring life. Life is what happens now, not when you've finished your to-do list.

---

### Closing

I asked myself what is the best way to remember what life is short means in a way that changes how you live. I think it's this: imagine a graph. On the y axis is how much you care about something, on the x axis is your age. The things that feel urgent but unimportant now they will shrink on the y axis by the time you're 60. The things that feel small and embarrassing now they may grow larger.

Don't optimize for what seems important now. Optimize for what will still seem important when you're old.

## Glossary

### Core Concepts

- **finiteness** the quality of having an end; not going on forever | *sınırlılık, sonluluk*
- **discrete** individually separate and distinct; countable | *ayrık, tek tek sayılabilir (continuous'un karşıtı)*
- **continuous** forming an unbroken whole; not stopping | *sürekli, kesintisiz*
- **heuristic** a practical approach or rule of thumb for solving problems | *sezgisel kural, pratik yöntem*
- **vantage point** a position giving a good view; a perspective | *bakış açısı, perspektif*
- **non-renewable** cannot be replenished once used | *yenilenemeyen*
- **junk** things of little value or usefulness | *saçmalık, değersiz şey (yiyecek değil, mecazi anlamda)*
- **peer pressure** influence from people of your own age or group | *akran baskısı*
- **prestige** widespread respect and admiration; high social status | *prestij, itibar*
- **persistent** continuing firmly despite obstacles or opposition | *ısrarcı, süregelen*
- **deferring** putting off to a later time; postponing | *ertelemek*

### Verbs & Phrases

- **to take (something) for granted** to not appreciate something, assuming it will always be there | *olduğu gibi kabul etmek, değerini bilmemek*
- **to compound** to grow by building on itself | *bileşik büyümek*
- **to deplete** to use up; to reduce the supply of something | *tüketmek, azaltmak*
- **to resent** to feel bitter or indignant about something | *içerleme, kızmak (içten içe)*
- **to cultivate** to develop or encourage something | *geliştirmek, beslemek, büyütmek*
- **to eliminate** to completely remove | *tamamen çıkarmak*
- **to defer** to put something off to a later time | *ertelemek*
- **to prioritize** to decide the order of importance | *önceliklendirmek*
- **to optimize** to make the best use of; to adjust for maximum efficiency | *optimize etmek, en iyi hale getirmek*

### Adjectives

- **ruthlessly** in a determined way, without mercy or hesitation | *acımasızca, sert biçimde (burada olumlu: kararlı)*
- **genuinely** truly; in a real and authentic way | *gerçekten, içtenlikle*
- **acutely** to a sharp and intense degree; very strongly felt | *keskin biçimde, yoğun şekilde*
- **trivial** of little value or importance | *önemsiz, küçük*
- **uncool** informal: not fashionable or impressive to others | *havalı olmayan, modası geçmiş*
