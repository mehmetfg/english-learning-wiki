## The Essay Tam Metin

I finally realized why, after years of thinking about it.

People can hold beliefs on two levels: a personal level and an impersonal level. On the impersonal level, they can hold ideas like "the earth goes around the sun" without feeling any particular attachment to them. On the personal level, they hold beliefs like "I am a good person" or "my group is right." These beliefs feel like a part of who they are.

The more labels you have for yourself, the dumber you become. If you call yourself a Democrat or Republican, a Christian or Muslim, a vegan or a carnivore whatever the label that label becomes part of your identity. And when an idea becomes part of your identity, you can no longer evaluate it objectively. You will defend it regardless of the evidence.

*(Kendinize ne kadar çok etiket yapıştırırsanız, o kadar aptallaşırsınız. Bir fikir kimliğinizin parçası haline geldiğinde, onu artık nesnel olarak değerlendiremezsiniz. Kanıtlar ne olursa olsun onu savunursunuz.)*

---

### Why Politics and Religion Are Different

Notice how much more upset people get about religion and politics than they do about, say, Linux versus Windows. Why? It's not because the stakes are higher. It's because political and religious views are usually part of people's identities.

When someone attacks your view on Linux versus Windows, they're attacking an opinion you happen to hold. When they attack your political views, they feel like they're attacking *you.* And when you feel attacked, you don't think you defend.

That's why even smart people become stupid when arguing about politics and religion. It's not that they're stupid. It's that the question has ceased to be an intellectual one and has become a personal one. Defending your identity is a different mode of thinking and a much worse one.

*(Siyaset ve dine dair görüşler çoğunlukla insanların kimliklerinin parçasıdır. Birisi siyasi görüşünüze saldırdığında, sizi saldırıyor gibi hissettiriyor. Ve kendinizi saldırı altında hissedince düşünmüyorsunuz savunuyorsunuz.)*

---

### The Cure: No Labels

The solution is not to have no opinions. That would be impossible. The solution is to not identify with your opinions.

Have an opinion on abortion? Fine. But don't let it become part of your identity. Call yourself "pro-choice" or "pro-life" and you've attached an identity to it. Now you're no longer thinking about abortion you're defending a team.

The way to have better ideas is to keep your identity small. Don't let labels accumulate. Don't call yourself a liberal, conservative, capitalist, libertarian, anything. These labels don't add information they only add noise.

*(Daha iyi fikirlere sahip olmanın yolu kimliğini küçük tutmaktır. Etiketlerin birikmesine izin verme. Kimliğine etiket yapıştırma bu bilgi eklemez, sadece gürültü ekler.)*

---

### What About Profession and Nationality?

You'll notice that most people who take pride in their work don't actually call themselves by a label. A great programmer doesn't say "I am a programmer" they say "I write software." A great writer doesn't say "I am a writer" they say "I write."

Saying "I am a writer" makes writing part of your identity. Then when your writing is criticised, you feel personally attacked. Saying "I write" keeps writing as an activity. You can be wrong about your current work without your identity being threatened.

*(Bir büyük programcı "Ben bir programcıyım" demez "Yazılım yazıyorum" der. "Ben bir yazarım" demek yazmayı kimliğinin parçası yapar. "Yazıyorum" demek onu bir aktivite olarak tutar.)*

---

### The Broader Point

The labels that do the most damage are the broad ideological ones. They cover so many topics that they end up being a filter through which you see everything. When you identify as a conservative, it affects not just your politics, but your views on art, science, food, music. The label infects everything.

The most intellectually honest people I know are the ones who are hardest to label. They might agree with conservatives on one issue, liberals on another, scientists on a third. They seem inconsistent because they're actually thinking about each issue rather than applying a master label.

*(En entelektüel dürüst insanlar, etiketlemesi en zor olanlardır. Bir konuda muhafazakârlarla, başka bir konuda liberallerle hemfikir olabilirler. Tutarsız görünürler çünkü aslında her konuyu ayrıca düşünüyorlardır.)*

## Glossary

- **label** a word or phrase used to describe a category or group; a tag attached to identity | *etiket (kimlik için)*
- **objectively** without personal bias or feelings; based on facts | *nesnel olarak*
- **to evaluate** to assess or judge carefully | *değerlendirmek*
- **to defend** to protect from attack or criticism | *savunmak*
- **stakes** the level of risk or importance involved | *risk, önem miktarı*
- **to accumulate** to build up over time | *birikmek*
- **ideological** relating to a set of beliefs or values | *ideolojik*
- **to infect** to spread through and affect negatively | *bulaşmak, sızmak*
- **inconsistent** not staying the same; seeming to contradict yourself | *tutarsız*
- **to identify as** to consider yourself part of a group or category | *kendini ... olarak tanımlamak*
- **to take pride in** to feel good about something you do or have | *gurur duymak*
- **mode of thinking** a way or style of thought | *düşünme modu*
- **master label** one overarching category that determines all other views | *ana etiket*
- **to cease** to stop; to come to an end | *durmak, bitmek*
