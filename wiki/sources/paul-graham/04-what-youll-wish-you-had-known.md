# 04 What You'll Wish You'd Known

## The Essay Key Passages

### Part 1: The Real Secret

If I had to reduce everything I have to say to a single sentence, it would be this: **work on hard problems.**

Not "work hard." That's a different thing. Hard problems are the ones that matter. The ones where figuring out the answer would actually change something. The ones where there is no obvious path forward.

*(Tek bir cümleye indirgemem gerekse: zor problemler üzerinde çalış. "Sıkı çalış" değil bu farklı bir şey. Zor problemler, önemli olanlardır.)*

Most of what you do in school is not work on hard problems. Most of school is learning existing solutions to already-solved problems. That's useful, but it's not the same as creating new solutions to unsolved problems.

---

### Part 2: What School Doesn't Teach

One of the worst things about school is that it gives you the impression that the answers are always known. Every question has an answer in the back of the book. Every assignment has a right answer. Every test has a correct solution.

But in real life, no one knows the answer. In real life, the questions that matter most are the ones no one has answered yet. This is completely different from school and most people are not prepared for it.

*(Gerçek hayatta kimse cevabı bilmiyor. Gerçek hayatta, en çok önem taşıyan sorular henüz kimsenin yanıtlamadığı sorulardır. Bu okuldakinden tamamen farklı.)*

School also gives you the impression that your job is to learn things and then demonstrate that you've learned them. In real life, your job is to figure out what's worth learning in the first place.

---

### Part 3: Keep Your Options Open

The most important thing to do when you're young is to keep your options open. Don't close doors before you've had a chance to see what's behind them.

The decisions you make early in life are rarely final. But some of them narrow the range of what's still possible. Every door you close is a door you can't walk through later.

*(Gençken yapmanız gereken en önemli şey seçeneklerinizi açık tutmaktır. Arkasında ne olduğunu görmeden kapıları kapatmayın.)*

How do you keep your options open? By not committing too early to a single path. By being genuinely curious about many things. By treating your life as an experiment, not a plan.

---

### Part 4: Find Interesting Problems

The best way to prepare for an interesting life is to find interesting problems. Not necessarily problems you can solve now just problems that seem worth thinking about.

When you find a problem interesting enough that you keep thinking about it without being paid to, that's a signal. That's your brain telling you something. Pay attention to it.

*(Ödeme almadan düşünmeye devam ettiğiniz kadar ilginç bir problem bulduğunuzda, bu bir sinyaldir. Beynin size bir şey söylüyor. Dikkat et.)*

The way to become interesting is to find interesting things. Read widely. Talk to people who have done unusual things. Ask questions that seem too basic or too large. These are usually the best questions.

---

### Part 5: The Problem with "Getting Good Grades"

Parents and teachers tell you to get good grades. But what they really want is for you to learn, be engaged, and develop good thinking skills. Grades are just a proxy an imperfect measurement.

And proxies become the target. When you start optimizing for grades, you stop optimizing for learning. You study what will be on the test. You avoid interesting but risky intellectual territory.

*(Notlar bir proxy'dir mükemmel olmayan bir ölçümdür. Ve proxy'ler hedefe dönüşür. Notları optimize etmeye başladığınızda, öğrenmeyi optimize etmeyi bırakırsınız.)*

The best students the ones who go on to do interesting work are usually not the ones who optimized hardest for grades. They're the ones who found something they genuinely cared about and worked on that.

---

### Part 6: The Nerds Are Right

Here's something counterintuitive: the kids who are labelled "nerds" who are obsessed with ideas, who seem socially awkward are often the ones who will end up doing the most important things.

Why? Because they care about ideas more than they care about appearing cool. Caring about appearing cool is a huge waste of energy. The energy you put into appearing cool is energy you could have put into something that matters.

*(Fikirlerle takıntılı, sosyal olarak beceriksiz görünen "inek" olarak etiketlenen çocuklar genellikle en önemli şeyleri yapacak olanlardır. Çünkü havalı görünmekten çok fikirlerle ilgileniyorlar.)*

Coolness is temporary. Ideas are permanent.

---

### Part 7: What to Actually Do

My concrete advice: work on something real. Not schoolwork something you actually care about. A project. A thing you're making. A problem you're trying to solve.

Don't wait until you know what you want to do with your life. You'll never know. Just start making things and see what happens.

The most successful people I know didn't have a grand plan at 18. They had curiosity, and they followed it. The plan emerged from the doing.

*(Tanıdığım en başarılı insanlar 18 yaşında büyük bir plana sahip değildi. Merakları vardı ve onu takip ettiler. Plan, yapmaktan ortaya çıktı.)*

Work on what's interesting. Be genuinely curious. Don't be too impressed by prestige. Keep your options open. And make things.

## Glossary

- **hard problem** a difficult, unsolved problem worth working on | *zor problem yanıtlanmamış, önemli*
- **proxy** a substitute measurement used when the real thing is hard to measure | *vekil ölçüt, yaklaşık gösterge*
- **to optimize for** to adjust everything in order to maximize a specific outcome | *... için optimize etmek*
- **to commit to** to decide firmly on a course of action | *bağlı kalmak, kararlı olmak*
- **counterintuitive** opposite to what you would naturally expect | *sezgiye aykırı*
- **to be obsessed with** to think about something constantly; unable to stop | *... ile obsesif/takıntılı olmak*
- **awkward** causing or feeling embarrassment or inconvenience | *beceriksiz, tuhaf, rahatsız edici*
- **to narrow** to make smaller or more restricted | *daraltmak*
- **engaged** actively interested and involved | *meşgul, ilgili, dahil*
- **genuine** truly what something is said to be; authentic | *gerçek, samimi*
- **intellectual territory** areas of thought, knowledge, and ideas | *entelektüel alan*
- **to demonstrate** to show clearly; to prove | *kanıtlamak, göstermek*
- **a proxy** *(bilgi alanında)* a measure used because the real goal is hard to measure directly | *dolaylı ölçüm*
- **to label** to assign a category or title to someone | *etiketlemek*
