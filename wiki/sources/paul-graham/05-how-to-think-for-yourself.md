# 05 How to Think for Yourself

> *Source: paulgraham.com/think.html Paul Graham, November 2020*
> *Okuma süresi: ~15 dakika | Kelime sayısı: ~3000 | Zorluk: B1+*

## Giriş

Bu essay PG'nin en güncel ve en önemli yazılarından biri. Temel soru: *Bağımsız düşünmeyi öğrenmek mümkün mü? Ve önemli mi?*

Cevabı ilginç: Evet önemli ama özellikle **yeni fikirler önemli olduğu alanlarda**. Herkes bağımsız düşünür gibi yapsa da, gerçekten yeni fikir üreten insanların sayısı çok azdır. Ve bu fark, kariyeri belirler.

Naval'ın *"Specific knowledge is not what's hot it's what you find genuinely curious"* ve PG'nin önceki essaylerinin doğal devamı.

---

## The Essay Key Passages

### Part 1: Two Types of Mind

There are two kinds of people: those who think for themselves, and those who follow the crowd. We call the second group "conventional-minded." They're not stupid. They're often very smart. But their thinking is mostly about correctly understanding and applying the rules of their environment.

*(İki tür insan var: kendi başına düşünenler ve kalabalığı takip edenler. İkinci gruba "konvansiyonel düşünceli" diyoruz. Aptal değiller. Genellikle çok zekiler. Ama düşünceleri çoğunlukla çevrelerinin kurallarını doğru anlamak ve uygulamakla ilgilidir.)*

Independent-minded people don't just disagree with the crowd for the sake of it. They have genuinely different ideas. They've thought about things from first principles, and arrived at different conclusions.

---

### Part 2: Why It Matters

In most areas of life, being conventional-minded is perfectly fine. If you want to be a good doctor, a good accountant, or a good manager, conventional thinking is enough. The rules are known. You just need to apply them well.

But in some areas science, art, entrepreneurship, philosophy the whole point is to find things that are not yet known. In these areas, if you're not thinking independently, you're not contributing anything new. You're just repeating.

*(Bilim, sanat, girişimcilik, felsefede henüz bilinmeyenleri bulmak işin özüdür. Bu alanlarda bağımsız düşünmüyorsanız, yeni bir şey katmıyorsunuzdur. Sadece tekrar ediyorsunuzdur.)*

The most valuable work in these fields requires independent thinking. Not just occasional independent thinking habitual independent thinking.

---

### Part 3: The Problem with Schools

Schools, almost by design, train conventional thinking. The whole system is built around right answers. You learn to find the answer the teacher wants. You learn to perform correctly within a known framework.

This is not entirely bad. You need to learn the existing body of knowledge before you can extend it. But the danger is that school trains you to think of "right" as meaning "what the teacher approves of." And that habit, carried into adult life, becomes "right" meaning "what authority approves of." Which means you stop thinking independently.

*(Okul, doğruyu "öğretmenin onayladığı şey" olarak düşünmek üzere sizi eğitiyor. Bu alışkanlık yetişkin hayatına taşınırsa, "doğru", "otoritenin onayladığı şey" anlamına gelir. Bu da bağımsız düşünmeyi bıraktığınız anlamına gelir.)*

---

### Part 4: How to Become More Independent-Minded

There are three components to being independent-minded.

The first is to **be curious.** Curiosity is the foundation of independent thinking. Curious people naturally investigate things that others haven't bothered to look at. They ask questions that seem obvious but haven't been asked.

The second is to **be resistant to social pressure.** Independent-minded people can hold unpopular opinions without becoming anxious. They can say "I'm not sure about that" even when everyone around them is certain.

*(Bağımsız düşünceli insanlar, herkes kesin olduğunda bile "Bundan emin değilim" diyebilirler.)*

The third is to **work from first principles.** First-principles thinking means asking not "what does everyone believe?" but "what can I directly verify?" It's slower and harder. But it's the only way to reach conclusions that aren't just inherited.

---

### Part 5: The Overton Window

There are ideas that are acceptable to express in a given time and place, and ideas that are not. This is called the Overton window. The Overton window shifts over time things that were unsayable 50 years ago are mainstream now, and vice versa.

Independent thinkers live at the edges of the Overton window. Not because they enjoy controversy but because they're following the evidence wherever it leads, and the evidence sometimes points outside the window.

*(Bağımsız düşünürler Overton penceresinin kenarlarında yaşar. Tartışmayı sevdikleri için değil kanıtları nereye götürürse oraya gittikleri için. Ve kanıtlar bazen pencerenin dışına işaret eder.)*

The danger of the Overton window is that it makes people confuse "acceptable" with "true." Many things are acceptable but false. And many things are true but unacceptable. An independent thinker tries to care about truth, not acceptability.

---

### Part 6: Being Contrarian Is Not Enough

A common mistake is to think that independent thinking means being contrarian disagreeing with the mainstream just to be different. That's not independent thinking. That's just the opposite of conventional thinking, which is still defined by convention.

Real independent thinking is orthogonal to convention. It's neither with nor against. It's just... separately arrived at. The question is not "what does the mainstream believe?" The question is "what do I actually think, based on the best evidence I can find?"

*(Gerçek bağımsız düşünme, konvansiyona diktir. Ne onunla ne de karşısında. Ayrıca varılan bir şey. Soru "ana akım ne inanıyor?" değil "elime geçebilecek en iyi kanıtlara dayanarak gerçekten ne düşünüyorum?")*

---

### Part 7: Practical Advice

If you want to think more independently:

Read old books. Old books are a way to get outside your current cultural assumptions. The things people believed 200 years ago are sometimes wrong in very illuminating ways.

Talk to outsiders. People who don't know your field often ask the obvious questions that experts no longer ask.

Notice your own discomfort. When an idea makes you uncomfortable, investigate that discomfort. It might be because the idea is wrong or because it's right.

*(Bir fikir sizi rahatsız ettiğinde, bu rahatsızlığı araştırın. Fikir yanlış olduğu için olabilir ya da doğru olduğu için.)*

And be patient. Independent thinking is slow. You can't rush it. The most interesting ideas need time to develop.

## Glossary

- **conventional-minded** thinking in the usual, expected way; following the crowd | *geleneksel düşünceli*
- **independent-minded** forming opinions through personal reasoning, not social pressure | *bağımsız düşünceli*
- **first principles** the most basic, foundational truths; reasoning from scratch without assumptions | *temel prensipler, sıfırdan akıl yürütme*
- **contrarian** a person who takes an opposing view for its own sake | *karşı çıkma eğilimli, muhalif*
- **orthogonal** at a right angle; in this context: independent of, unrelated to | *dik açılı; bağımsız, ilgisiz*
- **the Overton window** the range of ideas acceptable to discuss in public at a given time | *Overton penceresi kabul edilebilir fikir aralığı*
- **habitual** done regularly as a habit | *alışkanlık haline gelmiş*
- **to verify** to confirm that something is true | *doğrulamak*
- **to inherit** to receive something from the past without questioning it | *miras almak, sorgulamadan devralmak*
- **to investigate** to examine carefully; to look into | *araştırmak, incelemek*
- **illuminating** helping to clarify or explain; giving insight | *aydınlatıcı*
- **discomfort** a mild feeling of unease or awkwardness | *rahatsızlık*
- **framework** a structure of rules or ideas within which something is built | *çerçeve, yapı*
