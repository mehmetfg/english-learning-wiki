# 07 Hackers and Painters

## Giriş

PG'nin en meşhur essaylerinden aynı zamanda kitabının başlığı. Temel iddia şu: **Hackerlar (programcılar) bilim insanlarına değil, ressamlara benzer.** İkisi de bir şey yapıyor. Ve bir şey yapmak, bir şeyi keşfetmekten temelde farklı.

Bu ayrım kulağa teknik gibi gelse de aslında çok derin bir felsefi nokta: **Yaratmak ne demektir? Güzellik neden önemlidir? Nasıl öğrenilir?**

Naval'ın *"Code and media are permissionless leverage"* ve *"Building specific knowledge will feel like play"* fikirlerinin sanat ve yaratıcılık versiyonu.

## The Essay Key Passages

### Part 1: Hackers and Scientists

When I was in college, I was put in the computer science department. But I never felt quite right there. Computer scientists seemed to be mostly interested in proving theorems. I was interested in making things.

I later realized that hackers are closer to painters and writers than they are to scientists. Scientists discover existing things. Painters, writers, and hackers make new things. These are different activities with different goals.

*(Bilim insanları var olan şeyleri keşfeder. Ressamlar, yazarlar ve hackerlar yeni şeyler yapar. Bunlar farklı hedeflerle farklı aktivitelerdir.)*

The word "hacker" has a bad reputation because of the press. In popular culture, it means someone who breaks into computers. But in the programming world, a "hacker" means someone who is very good at programming and who loves it. A hacker is, above all, a maker.

---

### Part 2: Making vs. Discovering

Why does this distinction matter? Because making and discovering are guided by different instincts.

When you're making something, the goal is not correctness it's beauty. You want the thing you're making to be good. Good in the way a painting is good it works, it holds together, it has a certain quality that you can feel but might struggle to describe.

*(Bir şey yaparken, hedef doğruluk değil güzelliktir. Yaptığın şeyin iyi olmasını istiyorsun. Bir tablonun iyi olduğu gibi iyi işe yarıyor, tutarlı, tanımlamakta zorlanabileceğin belirli bir kaliteye sahip.)*

When you're doing science, you try to be objective, systematic, reproducible. When you're making art or code, you try to make something good which involves judgment, taste, and feel. You can't eliminate the maker from the thing they're making.

---

### Part 3: You Learn by Making

The best way to learn to make things is to make things. Not to study making. Not to take courses on theory. Make things.

This is how painters learn. You don't become a great painter by reading books about painting. You become a great painter by painting badly, then less badly, then eventually well. You learn from each painting what to do on the next.

*(Bu ressamların öğrenme biçimidir. Resim hakkında kitap okuyarak büyük ressam olmazsınız. Kötü resim yaparak, sonra daha az kötü, sonra sonunda iyi yaparak büyük ressam olursunuz.)*

The same is true of programming. The way to get good at programming is to write programs. Not to read about algorithms. Not to study computer science theory. Write programs, see what works, understand why.

---

### Part 4: Good Taste

If you make things, you need taste. Taste is the ability to distinguish good from bad. It's not innate it's developed. You develop taste by exposing yourself to a lot of things and paying attention to what you find good.

*(Bir şeyler yapıyorsanız zevke ihtiyacınız var. Zevk, iyiyi kötüden ayırt etme yeteneğidir. Doğuştan değil geliştirilir. Birçok şeye maruz kalarak ve iyi bulduğunuza dikkat ederek zevk geliştirirsiniz.)*

Hackers can have good taste. And it matters. A program written with good taste is more elegant, more maintainable, more likely to be right. Taste is not separate from technical quality it's part of it.

The best programmers I know have strong opinions about what good code looks like. They care about beauty in code. This might seem frivolous to people who think of programming as purely functional. But the most beautiful code is usually also the most correct code.

---

### Part 5: Copying as Learning

Another thing painters know that hackers are slow to learn: it's okay to copy to learn. The great painters copied each other constantly. Picasso copied Velazquez. Monet copied Turner. This is not plagiarism it's apprenticeship.

*(Büyük ressamlar sürekli birbirini kopyaladı. Picasso, Velazquez'i kopyaladı. Monet, Turner'ı kopyaladı. Bu intihal değil çıraklık.)*

When you copy to understand something, you discover why it was done that way. You learn not just the form but the thinking behind the form. Then you can go beyond it.

The same applies to code. Read good code. Copy good code. Understand why it works. Then write your own.

---

### Part 6: Working in Empathy

The best makers painters, writers, hackers make things for people. Not for themselves, not for critics, not for awards. For people who will actually use or experience what they make.

To do this well, you have to develop empathy. You have to imagine yourself in the position of the person who will use what you make. What do they want? What will confuse them? What will delight them?

*(Bunu iyi yapmak için empati geliştirmeniz gerekiyor. Yaptığınız şeyi kullanacak kişinin pozisyonuna kendinizi koymanız gerekiyor.)*

This is why the best programmers are often also good at other creative fields. They can imagine the user's experience. They can put themselves in someone else's shoes. They have empathy for code, for design, for the person on the other side.

---

### Part 7: The Importance of Day Jobs

One practical consequence: the best work often comes from people who have day jobs. The constraint forces creativity. You have to make something good quickly, because you only have evenings and weekends.

A lot of great art has been made by people who had to do other work to pay the rent. Wallace Stevens was an insurance executive. T.S. Eliot worked in a bank. Having a day job doesn't mean you can't be a great artist or a great programmer. It sometimes means you will be.

*(Birçok büyük sanat eseri kira ödemek için başka iş yapmak zorunda kalan insanlar tarafından yapıldı. Kısıtlama yaratıcılığı zorlar.)*

## Glossary

- **hacker** *(PG anlamı)* a highly skilled, passionate programmer; a maker | *hacker (PG anlamı): yetenekli, tutkulu programcı; yapıcı*
- **theorem** a mathematical statement that has been proved | *teorem*
- **innate** natural; present from birth | *doğuştan, içgüdüsel*
- **taste** *(here)* aesthetic judgement; ability to tell good from bad | *estetik zevk, ayırt etme yetisi*
- **elegant** pleasingly ingenious and simple | *zarif, şık (kod veya çözüm için)*
- **maintainable** easy to update and keep working over time | *bakımı kolay (yazılım için)*
- **frivolous** not having any serious purpose or value | *saçma, anlamsız, önemsiz*
- **plagiarism** presenting someone else's work as your own | *intihal*
- **apprenticeship** learning by working with an expert | *çıraklık*
- **empathy** the ability to understand and share the feelings of others | *empati*
- **constraint** a limitation; something that restricts freedom | *kısıtlama*
- **reproducible** able to be done again with the same results | *tekrarlanabilir*
- **to distinguish** to recognise the difference between two things | *ayırt etmek*
- **to delight** to cause great pleasure | *zevk vermek, memnun etmek*
- **functional** designed to be practical and useful, not beautiful | *işlevsel*
- **day job** the regular job you do to earn money | *asıl iş, günlük iş*
