# Les Misérables
### A Graded Reader A2 / B1 Level
*Based on the novel by Victor Hugo (1862)*

---

> **Reading Guide**
> This book uses simple English. Most grammar is **Past Simple** and **Past Continuous**.
> Sentences are short. Vocabulary is everyday A2/B1 level.
> The story has 10 chapters. Each chapter is about 5–7 minutes of reading.

---

## Chapter 1 The Prisoner

Jean Valjean was a poor man. He lived in France in the early 1800s. He did not have much money, and one winter his family had nothing to eat. He had a sister and seven children to care for. One night, he was very desperate. He broke a bakery window and took a loaf of bread.

The police caught him. A judge sent him to prison for five years. But Valjean tried to escape four times. Each time, the prison guards caught him. Because of his escapes, his sentence grew longer and longer. In the end, he spent nineteen years in prison.

When Valjean finally walked out of prison, he was forty-six years old. He was not the same man who went in. Prison had made him hard and cold. He did not trust anyone. He did not smile. His eyes were dark and tired.

He walked along the road with a yellow paper in his pocket. This paper was his identity document. It said he was an ex-convict a man who had been in prison. Everywhere he went, he showed this paper. And everywhere he went, people turned him away.

He knocked on the door of an inn. The innkeeper saw his yellow paper and said, "We have no room for you." He tried another place. The same answer. He tried a third place. The door closed in his face. Even a dog kennel refused him.

It was getting dark. It was cold. Valjean sat on a stone step in the town square. He did not know what to do. An old woman saw him sitting there.

"Go and knock on that door," she said. She pointed to a house on the other side of the street. "The bishop lives there. He will help you."

Valjean looked at the house. He looked at his yellow paper. He walked slowly to the door and knocked.

---

## Chapter 2 The Bishop's Gift

The man who opened the door was Bishop Myriel. He was an old man with kind eyes and a gentle voice. He was the bishop of the town of Digne, and everyone in the town loved him.

"Come in," said the bishop. He did not look at the yellow paper. He did not ask questions. "Sit down and have dinner with us."

Valjean was surprised. He sat down nervously. The bishop's housekeeper, Madame Magloire, brought hot soup, bread, and wine. She put out silver plates and silver cutlery the bishop's most valuable possessions.

"You don't know who I am," said Valjean. He put his yellow paper on the table. "I was in prison for nineteen years."

The bishop did not look at the paper. He just smiled. "This is not important. In this house, you are a guest. Please eat."

After dinner, the bishop showed Valjean to a warm bed. Valjean lay down, but he could not sleep. He kept looking at the silver plates on the shelf. He thought about how much money they were worth. He thought about how hard his life was. After a few hours, he got up quietly. He took the silver plates. He put them in his bag and ran away into the dark.

The next morning, three police officers knocked on the bishop's door. They had caught Valjean on the road. They brought him back with the silver in his bag.

"This man says you gave him the silver," said an officer. "Is that true?"

The bishop walked up to Valjean and smiled. "Yes, of course," he said. "I gave him the silver. But, my friend  " he turned to Valjean "you forgot the silver candlesticks. I gave those to you too."

He took the two silver candlesticks from the shelf and put them in Valjean's bag.

The police officers were confused. They left. Valjean stood alone with the bishop.

"Jean Valjean," said the bishop quietly. "I have bought your soul. I have bought it away from dark thoughts and from hatred. I give it to God. Do not forget that."

Valjean walked away. He was shaking. Something inside him had broken open. He sat down by the road and began to cry. For the first time in many years, he wept. When he stood up, he was a different man.

---

## Chapter 3 A New Life

Valjean changed his name. He called himself Monsieur Madeleine. He moved to a small northern town called Montreuil-sur-Mer. He had an idea for a better way to make black beads, a product that the town depended on. His method was cheaper and faster. Soon his factory was a success.

He became rich, but he did not keep his money for himself. He gave to the poor. He helped sick people pay for medicine. He built schools. He fixed roads. The town people loved him deeply.

After a few years, the town chose him as their mayor. Monsieur Madeleine accepted. He ran the town well and fairly.

But there was one man in the town who did not trust the mayor. His name was Inspector Javert. Javert was a police inspector. He was tall, serious, and very strict. He believed in law absolutely. For Javert, the law was everything. A criminal was always a criminal. A good person always followed the rules. There was nothing in between.

Javert looked at Madeleine sometimes with suspicious eyes. The mayor seemed familiar. He looked like someone Javert had seen before long ago, at a prison called Toulon.

One day, something happened in the street. A heavy cart fell on an old man called Fauchelevent. The cart was too heavy for anyone to lift. The old man was screaming in pain, trapped under the wheels.

Madeleine pushed through the crowd. People were watching, but nobody moved. Madeleine got down on his knees and pushed his shoulders under the cart. His face went red. His muscles shook. Slowly, he lifted the cart. Fauchelevent crawled out. The crowd cheered.

Javert watched this quietly. He remembered something. He had seen a man with that incredible strength only once before at Toulon prison. The man's name was Jean Valjean.

---

## Chapter 4 Fantine's Story

While all this was happening, a young woman arrived at the factory to look for work. Her name was Fantine.

Fantine was from Paris. A few years earlier, she had been young and beautiful, with long blonde hair. She had fallen in love with a student called Tholomyès. They were happy together for a while, and Fantine had a daughter named Cosette. Then one day, Tholomyès left. He wrote a funny letter and disappeared. Fantine never saw him again.

Fantine was alone with a baby. She needed money. She decided to go north and find work. But she knew that nobody would hire a woman with a child. She needed to leave Cosette somewhere safe.

On the road, she stopped at an inn in a village called Montfermeil. The innkeeper was a man called Thénardier. His wife sat outside while their two little girls played in the yard. Fantine watched the girls play and thought they looked happy and well-fed.

"Please," she said to Thénardier's wife. "Could you look after my daughter? I will pay you every month."

The Thénardiers agreed. They looked kind. Fantine left little Cosette there and continued north to Montreuil-sur-Mer. She found work in Mayor Madeleine's factory.

But the Thénardiers were not kind people. As soon as Fantine was gone, they put Cosette to work. They gave her old clothes and not enough food. Their own daughters, Éponine and Azelma, wore pretty dresses. Cosette swept floors and carried water buckets.

Every month, Fantine sent money. Every month, the Thénardiers wrote back asking for more. "Cosette is sick," they wrote. "Cosette needs a doctor." It was not true. They kept all the money for themselves.

Fantine worked hard in the factory. But one day, a supervisor discovered she had an illegitimate child. He fired her without telling Mayor Madeleine. Fantine needed money desperately. She sold her beautiful hair. She sold two front teeth to a dentist. She became thin and ill.

Finally, she turned to the streets to earn money. One night, a rich young man made fun of her in the street. She was angry and scratched him. Javert arrested her.

"Prison for six months," said Javert.

Fantine was terrified. "Who will send money for my daughter?"

Mayor Madeleine arrived at the police station at that moment. He had heard the story from witnesses. "Release this woman," he said to Javert. "She did nothing wrong."

Javert's eyes went cold. "You do not have the authority to  "

"I am the mayor," said Madeleine quietly. "Release her."

Javert released Fantine. Madeleine put her in a hospital. She was very sick. The doctors said she did not have long to live.

"I want to see my daughter," she whispered. "Please, Monsieur le Maire. Bring me Cosette."

---

## Chapter 5 The Inspector's Trap

Madeleine promised to bring Cosette to Fantine. But before he could go, something serious happened.

Javert came to his office. The inspector's face was strange almost embarrassed.

"Monsieur le Maire," said Javert, "I have made a serious mistake. I reported to the police in Paris that you were Jean Valjean, a dangerous ex-convict. But I was wrong. They have already arrested the real Jean Valjean. His trial is tomorrow in Arras."

Madeleine felt the floor move under his feet. An innocent man. An innocent man was going to prison because of his silence.

That night, Madeleine sat alone. He fought with himself for many hours. If he stayed silent, the innocent man would go to prison. If he spoke the truth, he himself would return to prison. The factory would close. The poor people of the town would suffer. And Fantine would die without seeing Cosette.

By morning, his decision was made.

He got into a carriage and drove all night to Arras. He arrived at the courtroom just in time. He stood up in the middle of the trial.

"Stop," he said. "Let that man go. I am Jean Valjean."

The courtroom went silent.

Madeleine Jean Valjean returned to Montreuil-sur-Mer. He went straight to the hospital. Fantine was lying in bed, very weak. He sat beside her and took her hand.

"I am going to bring Cosette," he said. "I promise."

At that moment, Javert walked into the room. He had followed Valjean back from Arras.

"Jean Valjean," said Javert. "I am arresting you."

Fantine looked up. Her face showed shock. She did not understand. She tried to speak, but suddenly she stopped moving. Her head fell to one side. She was dead.

Javert arrested Valjean and took him away. But a few weeks later, Valjean escaped from prison again. He disappeared.

## Chapter 6 Little Cosette

It was Christmas Eve. Snow was falling in the village of Montfermeil.

In the Thénardier inn, a little girl was working. She was eight years old. Her name was Cosette. She had dark eyes and thin arms. Her dress was old and too small. Her feet were cold in their wooden shoes.

The Thénardier daughters were sitting by the fire, playing with a beautiful doll. They had warm dresses and full stomachs. Cosette watched them and said nothing.

"Go and get water from the well," said Madame Thénardier. She gave Cosette a heavy bucket.

Cosette took the bucket and went out into the dark. The well was at the edge of the forest. It was a long walk. She was frightened of the dark, but she did not cry. She filled the bucket. It was very heavy. She could barely carry it.

Suddenly, a large hand took the bucket. She looked up. A tall man was standing next to her. He was old, with white hair and a serious face. But his eyes were kind.

"Let me carry that," he said. "It is too heavy for you."

He walked with her back to the village. Outside the inn, he stopped. He looked at the toy shop across the street. In the window, there was a big, beautiful doll with blonde hair and blue eyes. Cosette stared at it. She had never had a toy.

The tall man went into the shop and bought the doll. He gave it to Cosette.

She could not speak. She held the doll against her chest with both arms.

The man came inside the inn. He paid for a room. He watched how the Thénardiers treated Cosette how they gave her scraps while their daughters ate well, how they shouted at her, how they laughed.

In the morning, he went to Thénardier. "I want to take the girl," he said. "Her mother sent me."

Thénardier pretended to be sad. He said he loved Cosette like his own daughter. But when Valjean put money on the table, Thénardier agreed immediately.

Valjean took Cosette's hand. He bought her a warm dress and new shoes in the village shop. She put them on, and they walked away together into the forest. For the first time in years, Cosette felt warm and safe.

"Where are we going?" she asked.

"To Paris," said Valjean. "To our home."

"Are you my father?"

Valjean was quiet for a moment. "I am your friend," he said.

---

## Chapter 7 Paris

Valjean and Cosette came to Paris. They found a quiet, cheap apartment in the Gorbeau district, a poor and forgotten area of the city. They lived simply. Valjean taught Cosette to read and write. He bought her books. She was clever and learned quickly.

For a while, they were happy. Valjean was always careful. He looked over his shoulder in the street. He listened for footsteps. He changed their apartment when he felt unsafe. He was always running from the past.

One day, Javert found them.

Valjean saw the inspector's face at the end of the street. His heart jumped. He took Cosette's hand and they walked quickly then ran. Javert and his officers ran after them through the dark streets of Paris. It was night. The streets were narrow and confusing.

Valjean and Cosette ran into a dead end a street with no way out. Valjean looked up. There was a high wall. He found a rope, tied it around Cosette, and pulled both of them up and over the wall.

On the other side was a garden. And beyond the garden was a large, silent building. It was a convent a religious house for nuns.

By chance, the gardener of the convent was Fauchelevent the old man Valjean had saved from under the cart in Montreuil. Fauchelevent recognised him and was grateful. He hid them. He found Valjean work as an assistant gardener. He told the nuns that Cosette was his niece.

They lived in the convent for several years. It was safe and quiet. Cosette studied with the nuns. She grew from a small, frightened child into a calm, studious girl. Valjean worked in the garden and thought about his past. Sometimes, he thought about Fantine. He had not kept his promise to her in time. This still hurt him.

When Cosette was about seventeen, Valjean decided they needed to leave the convent. Cosette needed to see the world. They moved to a house on Rue Plumet, on the edge of the city, with a long garden and high walls. They lived quietly, very quietly. Almost no one knew they were there.

## Chapter 8 Marius

In another part of Paris, a young man called Marius Pontmercy was trying to understand his life.

Marius was twenty years old. He was the son of a colonel who had fought in Napoleon's army. His father had died when Marius was young, and his grandfather a royalist who hated Napoleon had raised him. As a young man, Marius discovered his father's true story. He found letters. He learned that his father had loved him very much. This changed everything for Marius. He left his grandfather's house and lived alone in a small room, earning very little money by writing.

Marius had friends a group of young political students called the ABC Society. Their leader was a serious and brilliant student named Enjolras. They talked about freedom and revolution. They dreamed of a better France.

Every week, Marius went to a park called the Luxembourg Garden. One afternoon, he noticed a young woman walking with an older man. She had long dark hair and big dark eyes. She was simply dressed, but she was beautiful. Marius looked at her. She looked at him for one second, then looked away.

Her name was Cosette. The older man was Valjean.

Marius came back to the park every week. He and Cosette looked at each other. They never spoke. But slowly, silently, they fell in love. Valjean noticed the young man's eyes following Cosette. He was uncomfortable. Cosette was everything he had. He moved them to a different part of the park, then stopped going at all.

Marius was desperate. He did not know where the young woman lived. He walked through the streets looking for her. One evening, he found the house on Rue Plumet by accident. He stood outside the garden wall and listened. He heard a voice her voice reading aloud from a book.

He came back every night. One evening, he called softly over the wall. Cosette came to the gate. They began to meet in the garden after dark. They talked for hours. They told each other everything. They were young and in love and completely happy.

Valjean did not know. He was growing more worried about the city. He heard news of political trouble. Groups of students were planning something dangerous. He decided to move to England. He told Cosette they would leave soon.

When Marius heard this, he was devastated. He went to his grandfather and asked for permission to marry. His grandfather was angry and refused. In despair, Marius went to join his friends at the barricade.

---

## Chapter 9 The Barricade

It was June 1832. A famous general had died, and Paris was angry. The poor were hungry. The students were ready to fight. They believed the moment for revolution had arrived.

Enjolras and his friends built a barricade in a narrow street in the Saint-Antoine neighbourhood. They piled up tables, chairs, overturned carts, and broken wood. They stood behind the barricade with guns. Other poor citizens of the neighbourhood joined them. They were not many, but they were brave.

Night came. The streets around them were dark and quiet. Then they heard boots many boots on the cobblestones. The National Guard was coming.

There was a prisoner among the students at the barricade. A man the students had tied up, because they suspected he was a police spy. It was Javert.

In the early hours of the morning, someone climbed over the barricade and joined the students. He was an older man with white hair and steady hands. He fought alongside the students. Nobody knew who he was.

It was Valjean.

He had received a note saying that Marius was at the barricade. He did not know Marius well, but he knew Cosette loved him. He went to the barricade.

The fighting was brutal. Students fell. The barricade held through the night, but there were fewer and fewer defenders by morning. Enjolras knew the end was near. He asked who among them had something to live for those men, he said, should escape while they could.

Enjolras asked the group, "What should we do with the spy?" He meant Javert.

"I will take care of it," said Valjean.

The students agreed. Valjean led Javert into a side street. He had a gun. Javert stood straight and calm. He was not afraid. He expected to die.

Valjean looked at the man who had chased him for twenty years. He put down the gun.

"Go," said Valjean.

Javert did not move. "You are a convict. I cannot simply  "

"Go," said Valjean again.

Javert walked away slowly. He did not understand. He could not understand. A criminal who showed mercy? This was not possible in the world as Javert understood it.

The barricade fell just before dawn. Soldiers rushed in. Most of the students were killed. Enjolras died last, standing tall against the wall.

Marius was wounded. He had a bullet in his shoulder and another in his leg. He fell. Valjean caught him. He lifted the young man onto his shoulders and carried him into the darkness of the Paris sewers.

---

## Chapter 10 Love, Truth, and Death

Valjean walked through the sewers of Paris for two hours, carrying Marius on his back. The tunnels were dark and wet and terrible. He did not stop. He came out at the river and found an exit. On the other side of the gate stood a man.

It was Javert.

Valjean stood with the wounded man on his shoulders. He looked at Javert. "Let me take this young man to his family," he said. "Then you can take me."

Javert was silent for a long moment. Then he nodded.

Valjean took Marius to his grandfather's house. Marius was alive, but only just. Doctors worked on him for many hours. He survived.

Javert walked away alone. He went to the river. He stood on the bridge and looked down at the dark water. He could not arrest Valjean. He could not let him go. These two things were both impossible for him to do. He was a man of the law, and the law could not explain what Valjean was. He had been kind. He had been just. He had saved a young man's life. And yet he was a criminal.

Javert jumped into the river. He was never seen again.

---

Marius recovered slowly. When he was well enough, he asked about Cosette every day. She came to visit him with Valjean. The two young people were finally together, openly and happily. Marius's grandfather, who had refused permission for the marriage before, had a change of heart. He saw how much Marius loved Cosette, and how kind and good she was.

They married. The wedding was simple and beautiful. Cosette wore a white dress. Marius smiled all day. Valjean watched from a corner of the room. He was happy. But he was also very tired.

After the wedding, Valjean went to Marius privately. He told him the truth. His name was Jean Valjean. He was an ex-convict. He had broken his parole. He had hidden from the law for many years. Cosette did not know any of this.

"She must know," said Marius carefully.

"I know," said Valjean. "But please let me tell her myself, in time."

Marius was troubled by this news. He was not sure how to feel. He allowed Valjean to see Cosette at first. But slowly, he began to limit the visits. He thought he was protecting Cosette from a dangerous man. He did not see that he was taking away the only person who truly knew her.

Valjean grew ill. He lived alone in his apartment. He ate little. He sat in his chair by the window and looked at the silver candlesticks on the shelf the bishop's gift. He thought about his life. He thought about Fantine. He thought about little Cosette in the dark forest, holding her new doll. He was at peace.

One evening, Marius finally learned the truth. He discovered that Valjean was the man who had carried him through the sewers. He had saved his life. He had been the one to find Cosette and rescue her from the Thénardiers. He had given his life to protect Cosette and to be a good man.

Marius and Cosette rushed to Valjean's apartment.

He was sitting in his chair. His face was pale. When Cosette came through the door, his eyes opened and he smiled.

"You came," he said.

Cosette sat beside him and took his hands. "Father," she said. She had always known that word was true.

Valjean looked at her. "I am so happy," he said. "It is good when people love each other. God made love for that reason."

He looked at the candlesticks on the shelf. "Bishop Myriel," he whispered. "I kept my promise."

He closed his eyes.

## Afterword Why This Story Matters

Jean Valjean began as a man who stole bread because his family was hungry. He spent nineteen years in prison. He came out full of anger and bitterness.

One act of kindness from a bishop who gave him silver instead of punishment changed his life completely.

The question Victor Hugo asks is simple: **Can a person really change?** And his answer is yes. But it is hard. It takes a long time. And society does not always make it easy.

The title *Les Misérables* means *The Miserable Ones* in French. Hugo wrote about the poor, the forgotten, and the desperate people of his time. He believed that poverty and injustice create criminals. He believed that kindness and love can save people.

Valjean's story is a story about second chances. About a man who chose, again and again, to be good even when it was hard, even when no one was watching, even when it cost him everything.

## Grammar Focus Key Structures in This Book

**Past Simple** for completed actions in the story:
> *He broke a bakery window and took a loaf of bread.*

**Past Continuous** for background actions:
> *Snow was falling in the village.*

**Reported Speech** what characters said:
> *She said she loved Cosette like her own daughter.*

**Modal Verbs** for possibility and obligation:
> *He could not arrest Valjean. He could not let him go.*

**Linking Words** to connect ideas:
> *Because of his escapes, his sentence grew longer.*
> *Although he was old, his eyes were still kind.*
