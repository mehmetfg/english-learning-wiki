# 06 Reading and Learning

## Giriş

Naval'ın en pratik ve en uygulanabilir bölümü. Bir yatırımcı olarak günlük rutininde en çok ne yapıyor? **Okuyor.** Ama nasıl okuduğu hangi kitapları, nasıl bir sırayla, hangi zihinsel yaklaşımla alışılmışın çok dışında.

Bu bölüm aynı zamanda **dil öğrenenler için doğrudan bir ayna**: Naval'ın öğrenme felsefesi, İngilizce okuma pratiğinin neden tek en verimli yöntem olduğunu açıklıyor. *"Reading is faster than listening. Doing is faster than watching."* bu tek cümle, İngilizce yolculuğunun manifestosu.

---

## Core Aphorisms Temel Aforizmalar

> **1.** Reading is the foundation. I am a voracious reader. Read everything you can.

> **2.** Reading is faster than listening. Doing is faster than watching.

> **3.** The number one thing that clouds our thinking is the social performance of our thinking.

> **4.** I read what I love until I love to read.

*(En önemli okuma tavsiyesi, bu kadar basit: sevdiğini oku, ta ki okumayı sevene kadar.)*

> **5.** I never feel like I have to finish a book. Books are not a chore. If you're not enjoying a book, just put it down.

> **6.** Read the original source, not the summary. You lose too much when you outsource your thinking to someone else's summary.

> **7.** Don't read for information. Read for understanding.

*(Bilgi için değil, anlayış için oku.)*

> **8.** A good book changes how you think permanently. A great book changes who you are.

> **9.** The most important books to read are the ones that were important 500 years ago and are still important today. That's how you know they contain real knowledge.

*(En önemli kitaplar: 500 yıl önce önemliydi ve hâlâ önemli. Bu, gerçek bilgi içerdiklerini anlatan işarettir.)*

> **10.** Any book that's worth reading is worth re-reading.

## Extended Passages Genişletilmiş Açıklamalar

---

> I mostly read non-fiction. I read a lot of science, math, philosophy, economics. I don't like reading fiction as much I prefer non-fiction where every sentence teaches me something real about the world.

---

> The best way to learn anything is to ask yourself: "If I had to teach this to a ten-year-old, what would I say?" That clarity forces you to actually understand it, not just memorise it.

*(Herhangi bir şeyi öğrenmenin en iyi yolu: "Bunu on yaşında bir çocuğa öğretmek zorunda olsaydım ne söylerdim?" O netlik, gerçekten anlamayı zorlar ezberlemeyi değil.)*

---

> I used to think I had to read everything cover-to-cover. Now I read three to four books at once, jumping between them. I read whatever I feel like. I treat books like blog posts I skip around.

---

> If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough. This is Feynman's rule. I use it constantly.

*(Basitçe açıklayamıyorsan, yeterince anlamamışsın demektir. Feynman'ın kuralı.)*

---

> The most important skill in the modern world is the ability to learn. Not just what you know but how fast you can learn new things. That meta-skill compounds more than any specific knowledge.

*(Modern dünyanın en önemli becerisi: öğrenme yeteneği. Ne bildiğin değil yeni şeyleri ne kadar hızlı öğrendiğin. Bu meta-beceri, her specific knowledge'dan daha fazla bileşik büyür.)*

---

> Don't read to impress others. Read to satisfy your own curiosity. The moment reading becomes a performance a way to signal intelligence you've lost the point.

*(Başkalarını etkilemek için okuma. Kendi merakını tatmin etmek için oku. Okuma bir performansa zekayı gösterme biçimine dönüştüğü anda amacı kaybetmişsin.)*

---

> I pick up a book when I feel like it. I put it down when I don't. I have no guilt about this. The goal is to love learning, not to consume a certain number of books per year.

---

> Arm yourself with specific knowledge, but also arm yourself with foundational knowledge. Foundational knowledge is the base of everything mathematics, logic, science, philosophy. If you understand the foundations, you can build anything on top.

*(Specific knowledge'la donan ama temel bilgiyle de donan. Temel bilgi: matematik, mantık, bilim, felsefe. Temelleri anlayan her şeyin üstüne inşa edebilir.)*

---

> The internet is the greatest gift ever given to the curious mind. For the first time in history, anyone, anywhere, can access the sum of human knowledge for free. The question is: what are you doing with it?

---

> I know that I don't know. That's the beginning of wisdom. Socrates knew it. You start learning fast once you accept how little you know.

*(Bilmediğimi biliyorum bu bilgeliğin başlangıcıdır. Socrates bunu biliyordu. Ne kadar az bildiğini kabul ettiğinde hızlı öğrenmeye başlarsın.)*

## Glossary

### Core Concepts

- **voracious** having a very eager approach; consuming things in large quantities | *obur, doymak bilmez (okuma için: çok ve hevesle)*
- **foundational knowledge** basic, core understanding that everything else is built on | *temel bilgi*
- **meta-skill** a skill that helps you learn and develop other skills | *meta-beceri öğrenmeyi öğrenme*
- **curiosity** a strong desire to know or learn something | *merak*
- **clarity** the quality of being clear and easy to understand | *netlik, açıklık*
- **compound** *(verb)* to grow by building on previous gains | *bileşik büyümek*
- **chore** a routine, unpleasant task | *angarya, zorunlu sıkıcı iş*
- **signal** *(verb)* to communicate something about yourself to others | *sinyal vermek*

### Learning & Education

- **cover-to-cover** reading a book from beginning to end without skipping | *baştan sona*
- **Feynman technique** a learning method: explain simply to check understanding | *Feynman tekniği*
- **original source** the first, primary document or text | *asıl kaynak*
- **summary** a short version of a longer text | *özet*
- **outsource (your thinking)** to let someone else do your thinking for you | *düşünceni dışarıya yaptırmak*
- **memorise** to learn something by heart, often without understanding | *ezberlemek*
- **consume** to read, watch, or use something | *tüketmek (içerik için)*

### Verbs & Phrases

- **to put (a book) down** to stop reading and set aside | *bırakmak, kitabı kapatmak*
- **to skip around** to jump between different parts instead of reading in order | *atlayarak okumak*
- **to impress** to make someone feel admiration | *etkilemek, hayran bırakmak*
- **to cloud (thinking)** to make unclear or confused | *bulandırmak, karmaşık hale getirmek*
- **to access** to be able to get or reach something | *erişmek*
