# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 2: Abraham the Jew
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Neifile*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

**Giannotto di Civigni**, Floransa'da başarılı bir kumaş tüccarıdır. En iyi arkadaşı **Abraham** adında zengin bir Yahudi tüccardır. İkisi yıllardır birlikte iş yapmaktadır. Birbirlerine saygı duyarlar ve güvenirler.

Ama Giannotto içinde her zaman bir şey taşımaktadır: Abraham'ın Hristiyan olmasını istemektedir. Çünkü onu çok iyi biri olarak görür ve onun cennete gitmesini ister.

Giannotto yıllarca konuşur, tartışır, açıklar. Abraham her seferinde akıllıca karşılık verir ama dönmez. Sonunda bir gün Abraham der ki: "Tamam. Roma'ya gidip Papa'yı ve din adamlarını göreceğim. Gördüklerime göre karar vereceğim."

Giannotto duyunca içi sıkışır. Roma'yı bilmektedir. Orada din adamları arasında rüşvet, yalan, ahlaksızlık ve kibir vardır. "Eğer Abraham Roma'yı görürse asla Hristiyan olmaz," diye düşünür.

Abraham gider. Roma'da birkaç ay kalır. Her yeri gezer. Papayı, kardinalları, piskoposları gözlemler. Gördükleri onu şaşırtır: açgözlülük, tembellik, günah... Kilise içinde dünyanın en kötü insanları yaşamaktadır.

Abraham Floransa'ya döner. Giannotto endişeyle karşılar onu.

Abraham şöyle der: "Roma'da çok ilginç şeyler gördüm. Her türlü günah, her türlü kötülük. Ama şunu da gördüm: bu kadar yozlaşmaya rağmen Hristiyanlık büyüyor, güçleniyor, her yere yayılıyor. Bu ancak Tanrı'nın eseri olabilir. O yüzden Hristiyan olmak istiyorum."

Ve Abraham vaftiz olur. Giannotto hem güler hem ağlar.

**Hikâyenin özü:** En güçlü argüman, iyi anlatılmış bir fikir değil, gerçeğin kendisidir. Abraham'ı Hristiyan yapan, Giannotto'nun sözleri değil, Roma'da gördükleridir.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: Two Old Friends

In the city of Florence, there was a cloth merchant named **Giannotto di Civigni**. He was a successful man. He was kind and a good Christian. His best friend was a Jew named **Abraham**. Abraham was a successful merchant too. He was honest and very intelligent. The two men trusted each other completely.

Giannotto liked Abraham very much. But one thing was always on his mind. He thought: *Abraham is a good man. He is kinder than many Christians I know. But he is not a Christian. If he dies without becoming one, he cannot go to heaven.*

Giannotto could not accept this. He decided to talk to his friend.

For many years, Giannotto talked about Christianity. He was patient. He explained things gently. He quoted the Bible. He told stories about the saints. He was never angry.

Abraham listened carefully. He was a fair man and a good debater. He gave good answers. He showed Giannotto where his arguments were weak. But the two friends always respected each other. The conversations never became fights.

After many years, Abraham said one day: "My friend, you have talked about this topic for a very long time. I have heard all your arguments. They are not bad. But I must see things with my own eyes. I will go to Rome. I want to see the Pope and the leaders of your church. I will observe them myself. Then I will give you my answer."

---

### Part Two: A Journey to Worry About

Giannotto heard this and felt cold inside.

He knew Rome well. He had been there for business. He knew what went on inside the church. He knew that some cardinals were greedy men. Some bishops sold holy favors for money. Some priests lived like kings. He knew the gossip and the scandals.

*If Abraham sees all that*, Giannotto thought sadly, *he will never become a Christian. He will come back and laugh in my face.*

But there was nothing Giannotto could say. He could not tell his friend, "Do not look too carefully." That would be dishonest. So he said goodbye and waited.

---

### Part Three: What Abraham Saw in Rome

Abraham traveled to Rome. He was a careful and patient observer. He watched everything.

He watched the Pope during ceremonies. He watched cardinals walk through the streets in expensive clothes. He went to dinner parties where priests ate and drank like kings. He saw church officials argue over money. He saw men in holy robes lie and cheat each other. He heard stories of secret lives and hidden sins.

Abraham took all of this in quietly. He said nothing to anyone. Then, after several months, he traveled back to Florence.

---

### Part Four: The Surprise

Giannotto came to meet him at the door. He looked at his friend's face, trying to read his decision.

"Well?" he said. "What did you think of Rome?"

Abraham sat down. "Rome," he said slowly, "is a city of sin. I have never seen so much lying, so much greed, so much pride, and so much cruelty in one place. And most of it was inside the church."

Giannotto put his head down. "So," he said quietly, "you will not become a Christian."

"Wrong," said Abraham. "I will become a Christian this week."

Giannotto looked up. His eyes were wide. "I do not understand."

Abraham smiled. He was a practical man. He liked logic. "Here is my logic," he said. "This church is full of corrupt and dishonest men. An institution like that should collapse. In a few years, it should disappear. But it is not disappearing. Every year it grows stronger. It spreads to new countries, new people, new lands. There is only one explanation for this: **God himself is protecting it**. God must love this church, even with all its faults. And if God loves it, then it is the true faith."

Giannotto stared at his friend. He had no words.

That same week, Abraham was baptized. He took a Christian name. He became one of the most genuine and thoughtful Christians in all of Florence.

And Giannotto finally stopped arguing. He did not need to anymore.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **merchant** *(noun)* a person who buys and sells goods, especially in large amounts, to make money.
2. **debate** *(verb)* to argue about a topic in a careful and organised way, giving reasons for your opinion.
3. **convert** *(verb)* to change from one religion to another; the person who changes is called a "convert."
4. **corrupt** *(adjective)* dishonest and willing to act against the rules for personal benefit, especially when talking about powerful people.
5. **scandal** *(noun)* a shocking event or piece of news about someone's bad behaviour, especially when many people hear about it.
6. **greed** *(noun)* the strong desire to have more money or things than you actually need.
7. **institution** *(noun)* a large and important organisation in society, such as a church, a government, or a school.
8. **logic** *(noun)* a careful way of thinking about something step by step to reach a conclusion.
9. **baptize** *(verb)* to put water on someone as a religious ceremony, welcoming them into the Christian church.
10. **genuine** *(adjective)* truly real and honest, not pretending; when a feeling is genuine, it comes from the heart.
