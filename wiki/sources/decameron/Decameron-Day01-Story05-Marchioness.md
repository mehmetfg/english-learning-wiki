# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 5: The Marchioness and the Chicken Dinner
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Fiammetta*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

**Montferrat Markisi**, haçlı seferine gitmiştir. Karısı, **Montferrat Markizi**, yalnız kalmıştır. Markizi'nin güzelliği ve zekâsı tüm İtalya'da konuşulmaktadır.

**Fransa Kralı** bu kadını duymaktadır. Markis'in seferde olduğunu, karısının yalnız olduğunu öğrenir. "Cenova'ya giderken yolum o bölgeden geçebilir," diye düşünür. "Onu ziyaret edebilirim."

Markizi haberi alır. Ne demek istediğini hemen anlar: "Kral bana sahip olmak istiyor. Kocam yok. Beni kolay bir hedef olarak görüyor."

Ama Markizi ne kaçar ne de panikler. Çok zekidir ve bir plan yapar.

Yemeği hazırlatır. Ama sofrada her yemek tavuktan yapılmıştır: tavuk çorbası, tavuk kebabı, tavuk salatası, tavuk turta. Sadece tavuk.

Kral şaşırır: "Efendim, bu bölgede sadece tavuk mu var?"

Markizi sakin ve kibarca şöyle der: "Efendim, burada her tavuk birbirinin aynıdır. Farklı görünebilirler ama hepsi aynı şeydir."

Kral durur. Düşünür. Anlar. Markizi ona şunu söylemektedir: "Sizin için tüm kadınlar aynıdır — hepsi birer tavuktur. Ve ben de onların biriyim. Ama bu benim için bir hakarettir."

Kral yüzü kızarmış, sessizce bakar. Kadının zekâsı karşısında saygı duyar. O gece saygıyla ayrılır ve bir daha bu konuyu açmaz.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Bir kadın, kılıç kullanmadan bir kralı durdurur. Tek bir sofra ve tek bir cümleyle.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: A King Hears a Name

The King of France was a powerful and proud man. One winter, there was a great feast at his court. Many nobles were there. They were going to join the Crusade — the holy war in the East. One of the bravest men at the table was the **Marquis of Montferrat**. He had already promised his sword and his life to the Crusade.

While the men ate and talked about battles, someone mentioned the Marquis's wife. People said she was the most beautiful woman in all of Italy. And she was as intelligent as she was beautiful.

The King listened. He felt a sudden, strong curiosity.

"This Marquis is going to the Holy Land," the King thought. "His wife will be alone at home. I could travel through that region on my way to Genoa. Just to pay my respects. Just to visit."

He ordered his messengers to send a letter to the castle.

---

### Part Two: The Invitation She Did Not Want

The **Marchioness of Montferrat** received the King's letter. She was a clever woman. She read the message slowly.

*The King is not coming to see the castle*, she thought. *He is coming to see me. He thinks that because my husband is away, I am somehow easy to approach.*

She did not panic. She thought carefully. She needed to say something to the King — something clear and polite. She could not insult a king. That would be dangerous. But she needed to be understood completely.

She had an idea.

She called her cook. "Prepare a great dinner for the King," she said. "Use only chicken. Every single dish — only chicken. Chicken soup, roasted chicken, chicken pie, chicken salad. Only chicken."

The cook looked confused. "My lady? We have pheasants, lamb, fresh fish—"

"Only chicken," said the Marchioness.

---

### Part Three: The Dinner

The King arrived at the castle. The Marchioness received him graciously. She was dressed beautifully. She spoke with warmth and intelligence. She was everything the stories had said and more.

*She is wonderful*, the King thought.

They sat at the long table. The servants brought the first dish: a bowl of clear chicken soup. The King drank it with pleasure. Then came the second: roasted chicken with fresh herbs. The King ate happily. Then a third: chicken pastries. Then a fourth: cold chicken salad. Then a fifth: thick chicken stew.

The King was confused. He looked at all the plates. He looked at the Marchioness.

"My lady," he said with a small laugh, "tell me — do you have only hens in this part of the world? I see nothing else on this table."

The Marchioness looked at him calmly. She said: "My lord, the hens here all look different. Different sizes, different colors. But in the end, they are all hens. A hen is a hen."

---

### Part Four: Understanding

The King put down his spoon.

He was a proud man. But he was also an intelligent one. He let the sentence sit in his mind.

A hen is a hen.

Women look different, have different names, different faces, different titles. But to a man like the King — a man who travels and takes what he wants — they are all the same. They are all just hens.

And the Marchioness was telling him: *I know why you came here. And I am not interested in being one of your hens.*

She had said this without anger. Without insult. With a calm face, a metaphor, and a dinner of chickens.

The King looked at her with new eyes. He felt something he did not often feel: real admiration, mixed with a little shame.

He said nothing more about the matter. The rest of the dinner was pleasant — they talked about politics and the Crusade and the weather.

The next morning, the King left early. He thanked the Marchioness for her hospitality. He said she was, in every way, a woman of honour.

He meant it.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **marquis** *(noun)* a European noble title, one level below a duke; a marchioness is his wife.
2. **crusade** *(noun)* a holy war fought by Christians in the Middle Ages to take control of Jerusalem.
3. **feast** *(noun)* a large and special meal, usually to celebrate something important.
4. **gracious** *(adjective)* polite, kind, and elegant in a way that shows good manners and respect.
5. **metaphor** *(noun)* a way of describing something by saying it is something else, to make a comparison; "a hen is a hen" is a metaphor.
6. **hospitality** *(noun)* the friendly and generous way you welcome guests into your home and look after them.
7. **admiration** *(noun)* a feeling of great respect and liking for someone because of what they can do or who they are.
8. **approach** *(verb)* to move toward someone or something; also used to mean "to try to talk to someone for a personal reason."
9. **confuse** *(verb)* to make someone feel uncertain or unable to understand something clearly.
10. **roast** *(verb/adjective)* to cook meat in an oven with heat; roasted chicken is chicken cooked this way until it is brown.
