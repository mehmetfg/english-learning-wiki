# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 4: The Monk and the Girl
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Dioneo*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

Geniş ve zengin bir manastırda genç ve zeki bir rahip yaşar. Manastırın başında **Abbot** (başrahip) bulunmaktadır — ciddi, saygın ve herkesin korktuğu biri.

Bir bahar günü, manastıra yiyecek getiren genç ve güzel bir köylü kızı gelir. Genç rahip kızı görür ve kalbinde sıcak, rahatsız edici bir his duyar. Kızla her hafta konuşur. Bir gün öbür rahipler uyurken kızı manastırın bahçesine, sonra da kendi hücresine davet eder.

Abbot, hücrenin kapalı kaldığını fark eder ve şüphelenir. Kapıya gelir, içeriden sesler duyar. Öfkelenir. "Sabah bu rahibi cezalandıracağım," diye düşünür ve odaya döner.

Genç rahip bunu görür ve hızla düşünür. "Abbot da bir insandır. Belki o da kızla tanışmak ister." Kızı orada bırakarak dışarı çıkar, hücresini kilitler ve manastır bahçesine gider. Bekler.

Biraz sonra Abbot sessizce gelir ve genç rahibe ait olan hücreye girer. İçeride bir süre kalır. Sonra çıkar.

Sabah Abbot, rahibi çağırır. "Seni cezalandıracağım," der. Ama genç rahip sakin bakar ve gülümser. Abbot durur. Rahibin gözleri her şeyi söylemektedir. Abbot öksürür, bakışlarını çevirir ve şöyle der: "Bir daha olmaz, tamam mı?"

"Tabii, Pederim," der genç rahip.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Güç, bilgi ile dengelenir. Bilen, cezalandırılamaz. Boccaccio burada din adamlarının ikiyüzlülüğünü gülerek eleştirir — ama hiçbir vaaz vermeden.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: A Beautiful Visitor

In a certain country, there was a large and wealthy monastery. Its walls were high and its gardens were peaceful. Inside, many monks lived and prayed and worked. The head of this monastery was an old man called the **Abbot**. He was serious and respected. Everyone inside the walls was a little afraid of him.

Among the young monks, one was especially clever and ambitious. He prayed well. He worked hard. Everyone expected great things from him.

One spring morning, a young woman came to the monastery gate. She was the daughter of a local farmer. Her family donated food to the monastery every week — bread, vegetables, sometimes eggs.

The young monk opened the gate. He took the basket. He looked at the young woman.

She was very beautiful.

Something warm and troubling happened inside his chest.

"Thank you," he said.

She smiled. "See you next week," she said, and walked away.

The young monk stood at the gate for a long time after she left.

---

### Part Two: A Secret Guest

The young woman came back the following week. And the week after that. Each time, they spoke a few more words. She was friendly and warm. He was charming and intelligent. The conversations grew longer each time.

One quiet afternoon, when the other monks were resting after lunch, the young monk made a decision. He invited her inside.

"Would you like to see the garden?" he said.

She came in. They walked among the flowers. Then he showed her his room. One thing led to another. She decided to stay for a little while.

For a few hours, both of them were very happy.

But then the young monk heard footsteps in the corridor outside his door. He froze.

---

### Part Three: The Abbot's Suspicion

The Abbot had noticed something unusual. A young woman had come through the gate. But she had not left. He had been watching. The monastery gate was his responsibility.

He walked to the young monk's room. He knocked softly. No answer. He tried the door handle. Locked.

From inside, he could hear two voices — low and quiet.

The Abbot stood very still in the corridor. He listened. His face was serious.

*So*, he thought. *This is what is happening here.*

He was very angry. He wanted to punish the young monk immediately. But he controlled himself. *Tomorrow*, he thought. *I will deal with this tomorrow morning.* He walked back to his own room.

---

### Part Four: A Useful Observation

The young monk looked through the small window of his room. He saw the Abbot walking away. He thought very fast.

*The Abbot knows. He will call me tomorrow. He will punish me badly. But wait — the Abbot is old, but he is still a man. He has eyes. He heard a voice through that door. Perhaps...*

He made a decision.

He told the young woman to wait quietly. He unlocked the door, slipped out, and locked it again from outside, leaving the key in the lock. Then he walked away to the garden and sat under a tree.

A few minutes later, he heard quiet footsteps. He looked carefully from between the leaves.

The Abbot was walking toward the young monk's room. He moved slowly and looked around several times to make sure no one was watching. He took the key from the door. He went inside. He closed the door behind him.

The young monk stayed under the tree and waited. He waited a long time. Then the door opened again. The Abbot came out. He walked back to his room, looking at the floor.

The young monk stood up, walked to his own room, and knocked gently. "It is me," he said. The woman opened the door. Her face was pink. She said nothing.

The young monk smiled.

---

### Part Five: No Punishment

The next morning, the Abbot called the young monk to his room.

He sat in his big chair. He wore his most serious expression. He put his hands on the table.

"I know what you did yesterday," the Abbot said. "It is a great sin. It is against every rule of this monastery. I should punish you severely."

He stopped.

The young monk looked at the Abbot calmly. He said nothing at all. But his eyes said everything.

The Abbot looked at the young monk's face. He saw that the young man knew. He understood what those eyes were saying.

The Abbot cleared his throat. He looked at the table. He moved some papers around. Then he said:

"I should... remind you to be more careful in future. Do not let this happen again."

"Of course, Father Abbot," said the young monk. "It will not happen again."

He bowed politely and left the room.

Outside the door, he permitted himself one small, quiet smile.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **monastery** *(noun)* a building where men called monks live together, pray, and follow religious rules.
2. **abbot** *(noun)* the head of a monastery; the most senior monk who has authority over all the other monks.
3. **monk** *(noun)* a man who lives in a monastery, follows strict religious rules, and spends his time praying and working.
4. **corridor** *(noun)* a long, narrow passage inside a building, with doors on one or both sides.
5. **ambitious** *(adjective)* having a strong desire to succeed and become important or powerful.
6. **suspicious** *(adjective)* thinking that something is wrong or that someone is doing something bad, without being completely sure.
7. **severe** *(adjective)* very strict or very serious; a severe punishment is a very big and hard one.
8. **slip out** *(phrasal verb)* to go out very quietly and carefully, so that no one notices you leaving.
9. **permit** *(verb)* to allow something to happen; here used as "he permitted himself a smile" — he allowed himself to smile.
10. **hypocrite** *(noun)* a person who says one thing but does the opposite; someone who acts differently from what they teach or believe.
