# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 9: The Lady and the King of Cyprus
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Elissa*

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

Kıbrıs'ta bir kral yaşamaktadır. Bu kral çok zengindir, çok güçlüdür. Ama zamanın en ünlü korkağı olarak da bilinmektedir.

Ona kim hakaret etse, kral hiçbir şey yapmaz. Sessizce bakar ve geçer. Bir lord onu alenen aşağılasa, sessiz kalır. Bir yabancı ona hakaret etse, yüzünü döndürür. Bu yüzden insanlar bir söz çıkarmıştır: "Başına bir şey gelirse ve hiçbir yerde adalet bulamazsan, Kıbrıs'a git. Kral sana tahammül etmeyi öğretir."

Bir gün, bir hanım krala gelir. Bu kadın büyük bir haksızlığa uğramıştır. Onuru çiğnenmiştir. Her yerde adalet aramıştır ama kimse ona yardım etmemiştir. Son olarak Kıbrıs Kralı'na gelir.

Kral büyük ihtimalle yine hiçbir şey yapmayacaktır.

Ama kadın beklenmedik bir şey söyler: "Efendim, sizden adalet beklemiyorum artık. Bunun yerine bir şey öğrenmek istiyorum: hakareti nasıl taşırsınız? Bana öğretin. Çünkü ben de artık hakaretleri umursamayan biri olmak istiyorum. En iyi öğretmen sizsiniz."

Kral buz gibi donar.

Bu sözler ona bir ok gibi saplanır. Çünkü doğrudur. Ve doğru bir hakaret, yalan bir hakaretten çok daha derin yarar.

Kral o gün değişir. Kimsenin değiştiremedeği şeyi, bir kadının birkaç dürüst cümlesi değiştirir.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Gerçek cesaret, dışarıdan gelmez. İçerideki bir ayna gösterir onu. Bu hikâyede ayna, adalet arayan bir kadındır.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: A King Without Courage

On the island of Cyprus, there was a king. He was powerful and wealthy. His island was rich. His soldiers were many. His courts were grand.

But the King of Cyprus was famous for something unusual — and not in a good way. He was famous for his patience. But it was not the good kind of patience. It was the patience of a man who did not fight back.

If someone insulted the King, he said nothing. If a lord spoke to him rudely in public, he looked away. If an enemy laughed at his kingdom, he let it pass. He never punished anyone for disrespecting him. He never acted.

His people were embarrassed. His soldiers were frustrated. His nobles lost respect for him. Everyone knew: if you went to Cyprus and insulted the King, nothing would happen to you. Absolutely nothing.

A common saying appeared in that part of the world: "If someone truly wrongs you and no judge will help you, go to Cyprus. The King there will teach you how to bear anything without reacting."

It was not a compliment.

---

### Part Two: A Woman Comes to Court

One day, a woman came to the King's court. She was well-dressed but her eyes were red and tired. She had clearly been crying for a long time.

She had suffered a serious wrong. Her honour had been damaged by a powerful man. She had gone to her local judge. The judge did nothing. She had gone to a lord. The lord did nothing. She had travelled far to find justice. No one helped her.

Finally, she came to Cyprus.

She stood before the King. Her back was straight. Her voice was calm but her eyes were angry.

"Your Majesty," she said, "I have been deeply wronged. I have asked for justice in every place I could think of. No one has helped me. I have heard that you are a good king. So I came here."

The King sat in his big chair. He said nothing.

The woman waited. Then she continued.

---

### Part Three: The Arrow

"But I have also heard another thing about you, Your Majesty," she said. Her voice was still calm. She chose each word carefully.

"I have heard that you bear all insults without acting. That when people wrong you, you do nothing. That when enemies disrespect your crown, you look away. I have heard that no one, in all your years as king, has ever received a punishment from you for a personal offence."

The King still said nothing. His face showed nothing.

"Therefore," said the woman, "I have decided something. I will not ask you for justice anymore. I can see that is not possible. Instead, I ask you for something else: **teach me**. Teach me how to do what you do. Teach me how to bear dishonour, how to swallow insults, how to feel nothing when I am wronged. You are clearly the greatest expert in this art in all the world. If I can learn your skill, I will stop suffering — because I will simply stop caring."

She bowed politely.

The King sat completely still.

---

### Part Four: Something Broke

The woman's words sat in the King's chest like a stone.

He was not angry at her. He understood that she was right. He understood that she was describing him accurately. He understood that the "skill" she was asking to learn was not a skill at all — it was a failure. A coward's habit.

*The greatest expert in bearing dishonour.*

He stood up.

"Wait," he said.

The woman stopped.

The King turned to his judges and his officials. He told them the woman's case. He told them to find the man who had wronged her. He told them to bring justice that same day. He told them to make sure the punishment was public and clear.

They were surprised. They had never heard the King speak like this before.

But they did it.

---

### Part Five: A Different King

From that day, the King of Cyprus changed.

When someone brought an offence to his court, he acted. When a lord behaved badly, the King responded. When enemies laughed at his island, he answered.

He was not cruel. He was not violent. But he was no longer silent.

The saying — "go to Cyprus if you want to learn to bear insults" — slowly disappeared. A new one replaced it: "Do not insult the King of Cyprus. He does not forget anymore."

The woman who had shamed him into action became part of the story people told. She had done what no general, no advisor, and no noble had managed to do. With a few honest sentences, she had changed a king.

She asked to learn his skill. Instead, she taught him his duty.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **insult** *(noun/verb)* something rude you say or do to hurt someone's feelings or damage their reputation; to insult is to say or do this.
2. **honour** *(noun)* the respect that other people have for you because of your good character and actions; losing honour means losing that respect.
3. **frustrate** *(verb)* to make someone feel impatient or annoyed because they cannot do what they want to do.
4. **offence** *(noun)* something that upsets or angers someone, especially a rude action or comment; also a crime or breaking of a rule.
5. **coward** *(noun)* a person who is not brave and avoids difficult or dangerous situations; the quality is called cowardice.
6. **accurately** *(adverb)* in a way that is completely correct and true, with no mistakes.
7. **swallow** *(verb)* to accept something difficult or unpleasant without complaining; "to swallow an insult" means to accept it without fighting back.
8. **noble** *(noun/adjective)* a person from an important and high-ranking family in a society; also used to describe a good or morally admirable quality.
9. **public** *(adjective)* done in a place or way that everyone can see; a public punishment is one that happens in front of many people.
10. **duty** *(noun)* something you are expected or required to do because of your job, position, or moral responsibility.
