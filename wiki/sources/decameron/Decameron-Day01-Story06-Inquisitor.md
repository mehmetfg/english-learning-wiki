# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 6: The Inquisitor's Problem
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Emilia*

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

Floransa'da iyi bir aileden gelen bir adam arkadaşlarıyla yemek yemektedir. Sofrada çok lezzetli bir şarap vardır. Adam kadehi kaldırır ve gülerek şöyle der: "Bu şarap o kadar güzel ki, İsa Mesih bile içerdi."

Herkes güler. Bu basit bir iltifat, bir şaka. Ama sofrada oturan bir Fransisken rahip bu sözü yanlış anlar — ya da yanlış anlamayı tercih eder. Bu rahip aynı zamanda **Engizitör**dur: kilisenin sapkınlık (dine karşı söylem) avcısıdır.

Rahip küçük defterine bir şeyler yazar. Birkaç gün sonra adam mahkemeye çağrılır.

"Dedi ki İsa şarap içer. Bu kutsal varlığa hakarettir. Sapkınlık."

Adam sakin bir şekilde düşünür. Sonra şunu sorar: "Peder, kim yiyor Floransa'nın en güzel yemeklerini? Kim içiyor en iyi şarapları? Kim yaşıyor en rahat evlerde?" Cevap: din adamları. "O hâlde," der adam, "en iyi şarabı içmek ibadet mi? Çünkü rahipler ve piskoposlar tam da bunu yapıyor. Ve siz onları sapkın saymıyorsunuz."

Engizitör kızarır. Bu konunun büyümesini istemez. Adamı küçük bir para cezasıyla serbest bırakır.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Güçlünün silahı zaman zaman onun kendi zayıflığına işaret eder. Engizitör saldırmıştı ama elindeki ok kendi tarafına döndü.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: A Nice Evening, a Dangerous Sentence

In Florence, there was a man from a good family. He was witty and enjoyed good conversation and good food. One evening, he was invited to a dinner party with several friends.

The food was excellent. The wine was very, very good — perhaps the best wine he had ever tasted in his life.

He held up his glass and looked at it in the candlelight.

"My friends," he said cheerfully, "this wine is so good that even Jesus Christ himself would drink it with pleasure."

Everyone at the table laughed. It was a simple compliment to the wine. Nothing more.

But near the door, there was a man in church robes who was not laughing. He was a Franciscan friar. He was also an official of the **Inquisition** — the church office whose job was to find and punish people who said or did things against the Christian faith.

The friar quietly wrote something in a small notebook.

---

### Part Two: The Arrest

Two days later, the man received a message. He was required to appear before the Inquisitor.

He walked into a cold, official room. The Inquisitor sat behind a large table. He had documents in front of him.

"You said, at a dinner, in front of witnesses, that Jesus Christ drinks wine," the Inquisitor said. "This reduces the holy Christ to the level of a common drinker. It is **heresy**."

The man blinked. "I said the wine was so good that Christ himself would enjoy it. I was complimenting the wine."

"That is not how it was heard," said the Inquisitor. "You have offended the Church. There will be a fine. Perhaps worse."

The man said nothing for a moment. He looked at the Inquisitor. He was not frightened. He was thinking.

"Father," he said, "may I ask you something first?"

The Inquisitor nodded.

---

### Part Three: A Question About Food and Wine

"Tell me," the man said. "Who eats the best food in Florence? Who drinks the most expensive wine? Who lives in the most comfortable houses?"

The Inquisitor said nothing. He was starting to see where this was going.

"It is the priests," said the man. "It is the bishops. It is the friars. Everyone in Florence knows this. The Franciscan monastery has the best wine cellar in the city. The Bishop's table is the finest in Tuscany. At every religious feast, the food and wine are better than anything I eat at my own table."

He looked at the Inquisitor calmly.

"Now, if enjoying good food and good wine is a sin — then the priests, bishops, and friars of Florence are the greatest sinners in the city. But no one is calling them heretics. No one is writing their names in a notebook. Therefore, enjoying good wine is clearly not a sin. And my compliment to the wine was completely innocent."

He sat back and folded his hands.

---

### Part Four: Released

The Inquisitor sat very still. His face was red.

He knew the man was right. Or rather: he knew that if he pressed this case, the story would spread all over Florence. People would say: "The Inquisition arrests a man for saying the wine was good, while the bishops drink the finest bottles in Italy."

That was not a story the Inquisitor wanted to be in.

He cleared his throat. He moved the documents on his table.

"I see the matter is more complex than I first thought," he said carefully. "We will settle this with a small contribution to the church. Shall we say, ten lire?"

The man nodded pleasantly. He paid the ten lire.

He walked out of the building into the warm Florence air. He went to his favourite tavern. He ordered a cup of wine — a good one. He told the whole story to his friends.

They laughed for a very long time.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **inquisition** *(noun)* a church organisation in medieval Europe that had the power to investigate and punish people who said things against the Christian faith.
2. **heresy** *(noun)* a belief or statement that goes against the official teaching of a religion; saying something heretical can be very dangerous in a religious society.
3. **friar** *(noun)* a member of a Christian religious group (like the Franciscans or Dominicans) who lives a simple life and often travels to help people.
4. **witty** *(adjective)* good at saying clever and funny things quickly; a witty person always has a smart reply ready.
5. **compliment** *(noun/verb)* something you say to show you think someone or something is good or beautiful; "what a nice dress" is a compliment.
6. **offend** *(verb)* to make someone feel upset, hurt, or angry by what you say or do.
7. **cellar** *(noun)* a room under a building, under the ground, often used to store wine, food, or other things.
8. **witness** *(noun)* a person who sees something happen and can describe it to others; in a court, a witness tells what they saw.
9. **fine** *(noun)* an amount of money that you must pay as a punishment for breaking a rule or law.
10. **tavern** *(noun)* a simple place where people go to drink wine or beer and spend time together; an old word for a pub or bar.
