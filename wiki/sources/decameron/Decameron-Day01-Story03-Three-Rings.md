# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 3: The Three Rings
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Filomena*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

**Saladin**, Mısır ve Suriye'nin büyük sultanıdır. Savaşlar ve saray harcamaları onu borç içinde bırakmıştır. Para bulmak zorundadır.

Çözüm olarak **Melchizedek** adlı zengin bir Yahudi bankacıya başvurur. Ama Melchizedek akıllı ve dikkatli bir adamdır. Parayı almak için bir tuzak kurmak gerekir.

Saladin şöyle düşünür: "Ona şunu soracağım: üç büyük dinden hangisi gerçektir — Yahudilik mi, Hristiyanlık mı, İslam mı? Eğer İslam derse, onu kendi sözüyle köşeye sıkıştırırım. Eğer başka bir din seçerse, kendi dinine hakaret etmiş olur."

Melchizedek bu soruyu duyunca tuzağı hemen anlar. Ama doğrudan cevap vermez. Bunun yerine üç yüzük hikayesini anlatır:

Zengin bir babanın tek ve çok güzel bir yüzüğü vardır. Üç oğlu da bu yüzüğü ister. Baba herkesi üzmemek için iki kopya yüzük daha yaptırır ve her oğluna gizlice bir yüzük verir. Baba ölünce kimse hangi yüzüğün gerçek olduğunu bilemez — hepsi aynı görünmektedir.

"İşte üç din de böyledir," der Melchizedek. "Hangisi gerçek? Kimse kesin olarak bilemez."

Saladin güler. Tuzağı işe yaramamıştır. İkisi dürüstçe konuşur ve arkadaş olur. Saladin borcu alır, geri öder ve Melchizedek'e hediyeler verir.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Zekâ, güçten daha güçlüdür. Melchizedek'in bir cümle söylemeden verdiği cevap, tarihin en zarif iktidar oyunlarından biridir.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: A Sultan Who Needs Money

**Saladin** was one of the most powerful rulers in the world. He was the Sultan of Egypt and Syria. He was famous for his wisdom and his fairness in battle.

But even powerful men have money problems.

Saladin had spent a very large amount of money. There were wars, palaces, armies, and gifts for loyal soldiers. Now his treasury was almost empty. He needed money urgently.

He thought about who could help him. In his city, there was a rich banker named **Melchizedek**. He was a Jew. He had a great deal of money. But he was also very careful with it. He gave money to no one without a very good reason.

Saladin thought: *I need a way to get this man to give me money. I will set a trap.*

---

### Part Two: The Trap

Saladin invited Melchizedek to the palace. He was warm and friendly. He offered the old man tea and fruit. He smiled broadly.

Then he said, in a very pleasant voice: "My dear Melchizedek, I have always admired your wisdom. You are a man who has studied deeply and thought carefully. So I want to ask you a serious question. Tell me honestly: which of the three great religions is the true one? Is it Judaism? Christianity? Or Islam?"

Melchizedek heard the question. He was old and very careful. He understood immediately. *This is a trap.*

He thought quickly. Every possible answer was dangerous. If he said "Islam" — he was a Jew. If he said "Judaism" — the Sultan could use that. If he said "Christianity" — that made no sense for a Jewish man to say.

Melchizedek sat very still for a moment. Then he smiled.

"My lord," he said, "that is a very good question. It reminds me of a story. May I tell it to you?"

"Please," said Saladin.

---

### Part Three: The Father and the Three Rings

"Long ago," said Melchizedek, "there was a very rich man. He had one extraordinary ring. It was made of gold and had a beautiful jewel in it. The man loved this ring more than all his other possessions.

"He had three sons. Each son was a good man — kind, wise, and loyal. The father loved all three equally.

"When the father was old and about to die, he had a problem. He wanted to give the ring to all three sons. But there was only one ring.

"So he called a skilled craftsman. The craftsman made two more rings, exactly like the original — the same gold, the same jewel, the same shape. When they were finished, even the father could not tell which was the real one.

"The father called each son separately. To the first, he gave a ring in secret. 'This is for you, my son,' he said. 'Do not tell your brothers.' He did the same for the second son. And for the third.

"Then the father died.

"The three sons came together. Each one held up his ring. Each one said: 'Father gave the real ring to me!'

"They argued. They went to judges. But **no judge in the world could tell which ring was the original**. All three looked exactly the same.

"And so, my lord, it is the same with the three great religions. God the father gave His ring to each of His three peoples. Each one believes its ring is the true one. Each one has good reasons to believe this. But who can look at all three from the outside and say with certainty: this one is real?"

Melchizedek stopped. He looked at Saladin quietly.

---

### Part Four: A New Friendship

Saladin sat back in his chair. He was silent for a long moment.

Then he laughed. A real and genuine laugh.

"You are a very clever man," he said. "I had a trap ready for you. But you walked around it so beautifully that I cannot even be angry."

He told Melchizedek the truth. He explained that he needed money urgently. He asked honestly, as one man asking another, not as a sultan giving an order.

Melchizedek was moved. He appreciated the Sultan's honesty. He respected a powerful man who could admit when a plan had failed.

He lent Saladin a large amount of money, with no difficult conditions.

Saladin paid back every coin later. He also gave Melchizedek gifts and honours. The two men became close friends and stayed close for many years.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **sultan** *(noun)* a king or ruler of a Muslim country, especially in past centuries.
2. **treasury** *(noun)* the place where a government or ruler keeps its money; also the amount of money itself.
3. **trap** *(noun)* a clever plan to put someone in a difficult or dangerous situation; if you "set a trap," you prepare this plan.
4. **banker** *(noun)* a person who works at a high level in a bank, or someone whose business is lending and managing money.
5. **craftsman** *(noun)* a person who is very skilled at making things with their hands, for example a jeweller or a carpenter.
6. **jewel** *(noun)* a beautiful and expensive stone, such as a diamond, ruby, or emerald, used in rings and necklaces.
7. **possession** *(noun)* something that belongs to you; your possessions are all the things you own.
8. **loyalty** *(noun)* the quality of staying faithful and supportive to a person, group, or idea, even when things are difficult.
9. **certainty** *(noun)* the state of being completely sure about something, with no doubt at all.
10. **genuine** *(adjective)* real and honest, not pretending; a genuine laugh comes from a real feeling.
