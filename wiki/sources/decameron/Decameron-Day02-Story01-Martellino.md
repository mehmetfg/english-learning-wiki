# Decameron
## Day 2, Story 1: Martellino the Fake Cripple
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Neifile · İkinci Günün İlk Hikâyesi*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

**İkinci Gün** başlar. Yeni kraliçe seçilmiştir. Günün teması: **Şans ve Talih** — beklenmedik şeyler, ani düşüşler ve kurtulmalar.

İlk hikâye komiktir ama tehlikelidir.

**Stecchi**, **Marchese** ve **Martellino** üç Floransalı eğlendiricinin adıdır. Treviso'ya gelirler. Şehirde yeni ölen bir azizin cesedi vardır. İnsanlar mucizeler gördüklerini söylemekte ve her yerden kalabalıklar gelmektedir. Kilise girişi tıklım tıklım.

Martellino bir fikir bulur: "Ben sakat rolü yapacağım. Siz beni kalabalıktan içeri soktururun. Azizin yanında mucize ile iyileşeceğim."

Ve yapar. Parmaklarını büker, ayaklarını çeker, sırtını eğer. Gerçekten sakat görünmektedir. Arkadaşları onu kalabalıktan içeri taşır. Azizin önünde Martellino aniden kalkar. "Mucize! Mucize!" Herkes çıldırır.

Ama bir adam Martellino'yu Floransa'dan tanımaktadır. "Bu sahtekar! Onu sağlıklı gördüm!" der.

Kalabalık öfkeli bir kalabalığa dönüşür. Martellino'yu döverler. Tutuklanır. Yargılanır. İpe asılma ihtimali vardır.

Arkadaşları onu kurtarmak için bir yalan söyler: "Bu adam bir İngiliz diplomatın parasını çalmış." Şerif bunu duyunca Martellino'yu ayrıca sorgulamak için alır. Orada her şey karışır. Sonunda yanlışlık anlaşılır. Martellino uyarı ile serbest bırakılır.

Üçü Treviso'dan o gün kaçarlar.

**Hikâyenin özü:** İkinci Günün teması şanstır. Bu hikâyede şans önce aleyhine döner, sonra lehine. Talih bir tekerlek gibidir — döner.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: The Beginning of Day Two

The next morning, the ten young people chose a new queen. Her name was **Filomena**. She was serious and organised. She announced the theme for the day.

"Today," she said, "each story must be about **Fortune** — about luck, both good and bad. About sudden changes. About people who fall low and then rise again, or who almost lose everything and then escape. Fortune turns like a wheel. Let us tell stories about that turning."

The first storyteller was **Neifile**. She stood up and smiled.

"My dear friends," she said, "let me tell you about three men who had a very bad idea."

---

### Part Two: Three Travellers

In the city of **Treviso**, something wonderful had happened. A very holy man had died there recently. People said that after his death, miracles had begun. Sick people prayed at his body and felt better. People with bad legs walked out of the church. People with weak eyes felt their sight return. The whole city was talking about it.

Enormous crowds came from every direction to see the body of this new saint. The streets near the church were completely packed.

Among the travellers arriving in Treviso were three friends from Florence: **Stecchi**, **Marchese**, and **Martellino**. They were professional entertainers. They performed at courts and festivals across Italy. They were good at one thing in particular: **physical imitation**. Martellino was the best of the three. He could walk like an old man, move like a child, stand like a soldier, or sit like a beggar — whatever the performance required.

When they arrived and saw the enormous crowd, they realised: there was no way to push through to see the saint's body.

Martellino looked at the crowd. Then he had an idea.

---

### Part Three: The Plan

"I know how to get us in," Martellino said.

He explained his plan. Stecchi and Marchese looked nervous.

"This is a bad plan," said Marchese. "Something will go wrong."

"It is a perfect plan," said Martellino. "Watch."

Martellino was an expert at physical imitation. He had spent years learning to use his body for performance.

He twisted his fingers and bent them sideways. He turned his foot inward and dragged it along the ground. He pushed his hip out and bent his spine. He let his mouth hang half open. He let his head fall slowly to one side.

He looked completely like a man who had been crippled since birth.

Stecchi and Marchese stood next to him and began to shout to the crowd: "Make way! Make way! This poor man has never walked properly in his life! He has come from far away to ask for a miracle from the holy saint! Please — let him through!"

The crowd opened immediately. People were kind and moved aside. They helped carry Martellino carefully toward the front of the church. They brought him right up to the body of the saint, and they laid him down close to it.

---

### Part Four: The Miracle

Martellino lay still for a moment. He breathed slowly. He moved his bent fingers slightly, as if in pain.

Then, very slowly and very dramatically, he began to straighten up. His fingers opened. His foot pointed forward. His spine uncurled. He rose to his knees. He rose to his feet. He stood completely straight, with his arms open.

"I can walk!" he shouted. "God and the saint have healed me! A miracle! I am healed!"

The crowd around him exploded with joy. People fell to their knees and wept. People prayed loudly. People reached forward to touch the saint's body again in gratitude. "A miracle! We have seen it! The saint heals people!" The noise was enormous.

Stecchi and Marchese watched from the side. Stecchi leaned close to Marchese and whispered: "I cannot believe that actually worked."

But then something went wrong.

---

### Part Five: Recognised

A man near the front of the crowd looked at Martellino's face. He looked again. His eyes narrowed.

He turned to the person next to him and said: "I know that man. That is Martellino the entertainer, from Florence. I saw him perform there last summer. He could walk perfectly well. He was not crippled before he entered this church."

The neighbour told someone else. That person told someone else.

Within two minutes, the whole crowd knew.

The joy turned to anger very fast. Hands reached for Martellino. Someone hit him from behind. Someone else grabbed his coat. "Liar! He faked a miracle! He mocked the holy saint! He is a blasphemer!" The crowd became a mob. They hit Martellino from every side. They tore his clothes. They dragged him toward the door.

By the time the city guards arrived and pulled him out of the hands of the crowd, Martellino was badly bruised and his clothes were torn.

The guards did not free him. They arrested him and took him before the city's judge.

Stecchi and Marchese had disappeared into the crowd immediately. They were safe. But they felt terrible.

---

### Part Six: In Front of the Judge

The judge in Treviso was a serious and experienced man. He looked at the charge: a man had entered a church, pretended to be healed at the body of a saint, and had deceived thousands of people. This was a very serious offence against God, against the church, and against the city.

The judge asked Martellino to explain himself.

Martellino told the truth. It was a bad idea. He had done it as a joke. He was an entertainer by profession. He had not intended to offend anyone's faith. He was sorry.

The judge did not look satisfied.

Outside, Stecchi and Marchese were desperate. They needed to do something. But they had no powerful friends in Treviso. They had no money for a large bribe. They did not know the judge.

Then Marchese had an idea.

---

### Part Seven: The Rescue

Marchese went to one of the judge's senior officials. He pulled the man aside and said quietly, in a serious voice:

"The man you have in custody — I know who he really is. He is wanted in another city. A powerful English diplomat lost a large sum of money, and this man is suspected of stealing it."

This was completely false. But the official was interested. He went to the judge and reported this.

The judge, now curious about a possible international crime involving a diplomat, called Martellino to a separate private hearing. He asked about the English diplomat and the money.

Martellino had no idea what they were talking about. He said so, loudly and repeatedly.

The official was embarrassed. The judge was confused. The official who had brought the information could not explain where it had come from. The whole thing became a complicated mess.

At this point, a Florentine nobleman who happened to be visiting Treviso recognised Martellino. He came forward and told the judge: "This man is a harmless entertainer from Florence. I know him personally. He is a fool, perhaps, but he is not a thief or a criminal."

The judge looked at all the confusion around him. He looked at Martellino, bruised and apologetic. He decided the matter was too embarrassing to continue.

He released Martellino with a firm warning.

---

### Part Eight: Leaving Treviso

The three men met outside the court building. Martellino looked terrible — bruised, tired, with torn clothes. He looked at his two friends.

"You said it was a perfect plan," said Stecchi to Martellino.

"You could have said nothing," said Martellino.

They looked at each other. Then they all started to laugh.

They left Treviso that afternoon, quickly and without looking back.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **fortune** *(noun)* luck — it can be good or bad; "fortune turns" means your luck can change suddenly in either direction.
2. **crippled** *(adjective)* unable to walk or move properly because of injury or illness; an older word that is now considered unkind, but common in historical stories.
3. **mob** *(noun)* a large crowd of angry people who are behaving in a dangerous or violent way.
4. **blasphemer** *(noun)* a person who says or does something that disrespects God or a holy thing; blasphemy is the act itself.
5. **arrest** *(verb)* when police or guards officially take someone and keep them, usually because they are suspected of a crime.
6. **bribe** *(noun/verb)* money given secretly to someone in power so that they will do something in your favour; giving this money is called bribing.
7. **custody** *(noun)* the state of being kept by the police or guards; "in custody" means officially held and not free.
8. **diplomat** *(noun)* a person whose job is to represent their country in another country and help manage the relationship between the two.
9. **suspect** *(verb)* to think that someone has done something wrong, without being completely sure; a suspect is the person you think did it.
10. **apologetic** *(adjective)* showing that you feel sorry for something you have done; looking apologetic means your face and body show that you regret it.
