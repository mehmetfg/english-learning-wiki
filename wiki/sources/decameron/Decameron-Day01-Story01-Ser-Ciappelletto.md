# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 1 Ser Ciappelletto
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet (Konu Hakkında Kısa Bilgi)

**Decameron**, İtalyan yazar **Giovanni Boccaccio** tarafından 1348–1353 yılları arasında yazılmış bir hikâye toplamıdır. *Decameron* "on gün" demektir. 1348'de Floransa'da büyük veba salgını sırasında, **yedi genç kadın ve üç genç adam** salgından kaçmak için şehir dışındaki bir kır evine gider. Orada **on gün** boyunca, her gün her biri bir hikâye anlatır. Toplamda **100 hikâye** vardır. Konular aşk, kurnazlık, din adamlarının ikiyüzlülüğü, talih ve insan zekâsıdır.

**İlk hikâye (Gün 1, Hikâye 1)** Panfilo adlı genç tarafından anlatılır. Hikâyenin kahramanı **Ser Cepparello** (lakabı **Ciappelletto**), Prato'lu bir noterdir. Aslında çok kötü bir adamdır: yalan söyler, sahte belgeler yapar, kavga çıkarır, dolandırıcılık yapar, küfreder ve hiç kiliseye gitmez. Hatta İncil'de yazan her günahı işlemiştir.

Zengin bir tüccar olan Musciatto Franzesi, onu Burgonya'ya (Fransa) gönderir; orada zor müşterilerden borç toplaması için. Ciappelletto orada iki Floransalı kardeşin evinde kalır. Fakat aniden çok ağır hastalanır ve öleceğini anlar. Kardeşler büyük korkuya kapılır: Eğer Ciappelletto günahlarını itiraf ederse, kimse onu kabul etmez; itiraf etmeden ölürse, cesedi kiliseye gömülmez ve insanlar kardeşleri suçlar. İki yoldan da kardeşler zarar görür.

Ciappelletto onları rahatlatır: "Beni merak etmeyin. Her şeyi düzelteceğim. Bana çok kutsal, çok yaşlı bir rahip getirin." Rahip gelir. Ciappelletto **tam tersi bir itiraf** yapar: kendisini hayatı boyunca asla et yememiş, asla yalan söylememiş, asla kadına bakmamış, oruç tutan, kiliseye giden, dünyanın en temiz adamı olarak anlatır. Küçük günahları bile büyük bir pişmanlıkla anlatır ve ağlar. Yaşlı rahip onun gibi temiz birini hiç görmediğini düşünür.

Ciappelletto ölünce rahip, manastırında onu **aziz** olarak ilan eder. Halk cesedini görmeye gelir, mucizeler olduğuna inanılır, ona "Aziz Ciappelletto" (San Ciappelletto) denir. İnsanlar onun mezarına dua eder ve birçoğu duasının kabul olduğuna inanır.

**Hikâyenin temel fikri:** Boccaccio burada hem dinî kurumların safça inanışını eleştirir hem de "Tanrı, içtenliğe bakar, kişinin gerçek değerine değil bazen kötü bir adam bile, başkalarının iyi niyeti sayesinde aziz olabilir" der. Yani aslında bir ironi vardır: kilise, en günahkâr adamı aziz yapmıştır ve halkın duası yine de kabul olmaktadır. Bu çelişki, kitabın ana renklerinden biridir: **insan ikiyüzlülüğü, talih, dilin gücü, ve dinin sosyal görüntüsü**.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: The Frame Why These Stories Exist

In the year 1348, a terrible sickness came to the city of Florence in Italy. People called it the **plague**. Many, many people died. The streets were empty. Families did not eat together anymore. Friends did not visit friends. Even fathers and mothers were afraid to touch their own sick children.

In a church one Tuesday morning, **seven young women** met. They were all from good families. They were tired and sad. One of them, named Pampinea, had an idea. "Why do we stay in this city of death?" she asked. "We have houses in the country. The air there is clean. Let us go and live there for some time. Let us forget this sadness for a while."

The other women liked the idea. But they did not want to travel without men. So they invited **three young gentlemen** they knew. The men said yes happily. Together, the **ten young people** left Florence and went to a beautiful house in the green hills.

There, they made a simple rule. Every day, one person would be the "king" or "queen" for that day. And every afternoon, after lunch and a short rest, each of the ten would tell **one story**. Ten people. Ten days. **One hundred stories** in total. This is the book we call the *Decameron*.

The first day, Pampinea was the queen. She chose the first storyteller: a young man named **Panfilo**. Panfilo stood up, smiled, and began to speak.

---

### Part Two: The Worst Man in Florence

"My dear friends," Panfilo said, "I want to tell you a strange story. It is about a man whose life was full of sin, but whose death made him a saint. Listen well."

Once, there was a very rich merchant in Florence. His name was **Musciatto Franzesi**. He had business everywhere in France, in Italy, in many cities. One day, the King of France called him to come and work for him. Musciatto agreed, but first he had to fix his money problems in Burgundy.

The people in Burgundy who owed him money were not nice people. They were rude. They were dangerous. To collect money from them, Musciatto needed a man who was also rude, also dangerous a man with **no fear** and **no shame**.

Musciatto thought for a long time. Finally, one name came to his mind: **Ser Cepparello** from Prato. People did not call him by his real name. They called him **Ciappelletto** a small joke that meant "little hat" or "little flower." He was short and very well-dressed, so the name stuck.

Now, who was this Ciappelletto, really?

He was a **notary**, which means he wrote legal papers for a living. But his papers were almost always **false**. He wrote lies on purpose. He made fake contracts. He gave fake evidence in court.

He loved trouble. When two men were fighting, he made the fight bigger. When friends were happy, he started rumors to make them angry. If someone trusted him, he stole from them. He **swore** terrible words all day. He never went to church. He hated priests. He cheated at games. He drank too much wine and ate too much food. He treated women badly. He was happy only when other people were hurt.

In one sentence: **he was, perhaps, the worst man alive.**

But he was also clever, and he dressed well, and he could speak with a sweet voice when he wanted to. That is why Musciatto chose him.

"Ciappelletto," Musciatto said, "I need a hard man for a hard job. Go to Burgundy. Collect my money. I will pay you well."

Ciappelletto smiled. "I have nothing to do here," he said. "I will go."

---

### Part Three: A Foreign House, A Sudden Sickness

Ciappelletto travelled to Burgundy. He did not speak French very well, but that did not stop him. He arrived in a small French town and asked for a place to stay. Two brothers from Florence lived there. They were merchants too. They knew Musciatto, so they welcomed Ciappelletto into their house.

For a few weeks, Ciappelletto worked. He visited the people who owed money. He shouted at them. He threatened them. He used every dirty trick he knew. He collected some of the money, but not all.

Then, one cold evening, something nobody expected happened. **Ciappelletto became very sick.** His face turned grey. His hands were hot. He could not stand. The two brothers called a doctor. The doctor looked at him, shook his head, and said quietly, "I am sorry. This man will die in a few days. There is nothing we can do."

The two brothers were now in serious trouble. They sat together in another room and spoke in low voices.

"What are we going to do?" the older brother said. "This man is a guest in our house. If we send him away now, when he is dying, people will say we are cruel."

"Yes," said the younger brother. "But if he stays, it is worse! You know how he lives. You know the things he has done. No **priest** will give him forgiveness. No priest will listen to his confession. And without confession, the church will not let us **bury** him in holy ground."

"And if the church does not bury him," the older brother continued, "the local people will get angry. They will say, 'These two foreigners are hiding an evil man in their house!' They will come with sticks. They will burn our shop. They might even kill us."

"So if he confesses honestly, we lose. If he does not confess, we also lose," said the younger brother. They sat in silence. They did not know what to do.

Behind the door, lying on his bed, Ciappelletto **heard everything**. His body was weak, but his mind was sharp as a knife. A small, slow smile came to his thin lips.

He called the brothers in.

"Come here, my friends," he said softly. "I heard your words. Do not worry. I will fix this problem myself. Bring me the **holiest** old priest you can find the most respected one, the one nobody doubts. I will do the rest."

The brothers looked at each other. They had no better plan. So they went to a nearby **monastery** and asked for the oldest, most famous friar a man known across the region for his good life and his deep faith. The old friar agreed and came to the house that same evening.

---

### Part Four: The False Confession

The old priest sat next to Ciappelletto's bed. The brothers stood far away, so they could not hear, but close enough to see. Ciappelletto looked very small and very weak under the blanket. His eyes were soft. His voice was a whisper.

"Father," he said, "I want to **confess** all my sins before I die. I have not confessed for a whole week. Please forgive me even one week without confession is too long for a soul like mine."

The old priest smiled gently. "My son, when did you confess last time?"

"Father, I confess every Saturday. Always. Many times every Saturday I cry, because I am so afraid of God."

The priest was already surprised. Most men confessed once a year, or never. He thought, *This is a very holy man.*

Then Ciappelletto began. He spoke for hours. But every sin he confessed was **the opposite of his real life**.

"Father, I am ashamed. When I was a small boy, I sometimes spoke rudely to my mother. I still feel pain in my heart for that."

The priest's eyes became wet. "My son, that is a very small thing. God forgives this easily."

"Father, one time, I was so hungry that I drank water on a **fasting** day, before the time was right. I did not eat any food only water but still, I broke the rule. I have cried about it for years."

The priest could not believe his ears. He had never met a man so careful, so pure.

"Father, I must tell you the worst thing. One time, I was in a shop, and I **spat** on the floor of the shop. The shop was near a church. I am afraid that maybe, somehow, my spit went a little towards the church. I beg God to forgive me."

The old priest was now almost crying. *This man, dying in a foreign land, is one of the most beautiful souls I have ever met.*

Ciappelletto went on. He said he had **never eaten meat** on a Friday in his whole life. He said he had **never told a single lie** not even a small one. He said he had **never looked at a woman** with desire, not even his own neighbour. He said he had **fasted** every week, prayed every day, given money to the poor secretly. He said the only thing he ever felt was love for God.

When the priest asked, "My son, have you ever, perhaps, done anything bad with money? Have you ever cheated someone in business?" Ciappelletto opened his eyes wide.

"Father! How could you ask such a thing? I gave away most of my money to the poor. I worked only to feed myself simply, like a bird."

The priest now had tears running down his face. He **blessed** Ciappelletto. He gave him forgiveness for the tiny "sins" he had confessed. He promised him heaven.

"My son, you are a **saint**. I will tell my brothers at the monastery. I will tell everyone."

Behind the door, the two brothers had heard some of the conversation. They almost laughed out loud. They covered their mouths. They could not believe what they were hearing. *This man is the most evil liar in Christendom*, they thought, *and the priest believes every word.*

That same night, Ciappelletto died.

---

### Part Five: A New Saint Is Born

The next morning, the old priest went to his monastery. He spoke to the other friars with shining eyes.

"Brothers," he said, "last night, a true saint died in our town. I have heard his confession. I have never met a man so close to God. We must give him a great funeral. We must put his body in our church, in the place of honour."

The friars were amazed. They prepared a beautiful funeral. Bells rang across the town. Priests came in their best clothes. Candles burned everywhere. People filled the streets. They wanted to see the body of this holy stranger from Italy.

The old priest stood in front of the crowd and **preached** a long sermon. He told everyone about Ciappelletto's pure life: how he never lied, never ate meat on Fridays, never looked at a woman, never broke a single rule of God.

The people listened with open mouths. They cried. They prayed. When the body was carried into the church, the crowd pushed forward to touch his hands, his feet, his clothes. They tore small pieces from his shirt to keep as **holy** objects.

Soon, **stories of miracles** began. A woman with a sick child prayed at his grave; the child got better. A man who could not walk well prayed there; he felt stronger. People said, "**San Ciappelletto** Saint Ciappelletto answers our prayers!"

Every year, on the day he died, people came to his grave with candles. They called him *Saint*. They asked for his help in their problems. And many believed truly believed that he gave them help from heaven.

---

### Part Six: Panfilo's Final Words

Panfilo finished his story. The other nine listeners were quiet for a moment. Then he spoke softly.

"My friends, what does this story teach us? It is strange, perhaps even shocking. A man who lived the worst possible life became, after his death, a saint with miracles in his name.

"Some people will say this is a **scandal**. They will say it shows that the Church is foolish, that priests are easy to trick, that **holiness** is just a costume.

"But I think there is another way to read it. Perhaps God, in his great kindness, looks at the **hearts of those who pray**, not at the truth of the saint they pray to. Perhaps the simple people who prayed to Saint Ciappelletto received help not because Ciappelletto was good, but because their own prayer was sincere. God does not need our saints to be perfect. He only needs **our trust** to be real.

"Or perhaps who knows? the story shows us that words can build any reality. A clever tongue, even on a deathbed, can turn black into white. The whole world believed Ciappelletto, because he **spoke** like a saint. And the world will always be like this.

"So, my friends: be careful what you believe. Be careful how you judge. Sometimes the holy man is a liar. Sometimes the liar gives life to honest prayers. **The world is mixed**, like a glass of wine and water and only God can separate the two."

The seven women and the two other men sat quietly. Some smiled. Some shook their heads. Then the next storyteller stood up and the long, strange afternoon of the *Decameron* continued.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **plague** *(noun)* a very serious sickness that spreads quickly from person to person and kills many people in a short time.
2. **notary** *(noun)* a person whose job is to write and check official legal papers, like contracts and wills, so they are legally correct.
3. **sin** *(noun)* an action that is against the rules of God or against what religion says is good; a bad action in a religious sense.
4. **swear** *(verb, past: swore)* to use very rude or bad words, often when you are angry; also, to make a serious promise.
5. **confess** *(verb)* to tell someone, especially a priest, that you have done something bad or wrong, in order to feel better or be forgiven.
6. **priest** *(noun)* a man whose job is to lead religious ceremonies and help people in a church, especially in the Christian religion.
7. **monastery** *(noun)* a building or group of buildings where men called monks or friars live together to pray and serve God.
8. **bury** *(verb)* to put a dead body in the ground and cover it with earth, usually in a special place near a church.
9. **bless** *(verb)* to ask God to protect or help someone; a priest does this by saying special words and making a sign with his hand.
10. **saint** *(noun)* a very holy person, usually one who has died, that the Church officially says lived a perfect life and is now in heaven.
