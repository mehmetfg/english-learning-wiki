# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 7: Bergamino and the Generous Lord
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Filostrato*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

**Bergamino**, İtalya'da tanınan bir hikâyecidir. Şiir okur, komik sahneler yapar, insanları düşündürür. Adı her yerde bilinir.

**Can Grande della Scala**, Verona'nın efendisidir. Cömertliğiyle efsaneleşmiştir. Her gelen şaire, her gezgin müzisyene, her misafire kapılarını açar ve eli açıktır.

Bergamino bu haberi duyar ve Verona'ya gelir. İki hizmetçisi, üç iyi atı ve en güzel elbiseleri vardır. Büyük umutlarla gelir. Can Grande'nin sarayında gösteriler yapar. Lord güler ve alkışlar. Ama günler geçer. Haftalar geçer. Hiçbir hediye gelmez.

Bergamino'nun parası biter. Otel borcu birikir. Otelci en iyi elbiselerini borç karşılığında alır. Artık neredeyse eli boştur.

Bir gün Can Grande onu görür ve sorar: "Neden bu kadar üzgünsün?" Bergamino saygıyla bir hikâye anlatır: Ünlü bir şair **Primas**, cömertliğiyle bilinen bir piskoposu ziyaret eder. Ama o gün piskopos, hiç bilmediği bir şey yapar: cimrilik. Şaire hiçbir şey vermez. Şair aç ve elleri boş döner.

Bergamino durur ve Can Grande'ye bakar. "Efendim, bu hikâyede piskopos sizsiniz. Ben de Primas'ım."

Can Grande güler. İyi bir güldür bu. Sonra her şeyi telafi eder: borcu öder, elbiseler geri alınır, at ve hediyeler verilir.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Doğruyu söylemenin en güzel yolu bazen dolaylı bir hikâyedir. Ve gerçekten iyi bir insan, aynada kendini görünce güler, kızgınlanmaz.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: A Famous Storyteller

There was once a man named **Bergamino**. He was a professional storyteller and entertainer. He could make any group of people laugh or think. He could imitate voices and characters. He knew stories from every region of Italy. He was famous and well-respected.

In the city of Verona, there was a lord named **Can Grande della Scala**. He was known across all of Italy for one quality above all others: **generosity**. He gave gifts to poets and musicians and soldiers and strangers. He kept an open table — anyone could come and eat. His name was almost a legend.

When Bergamino heard about Can Grande, he made a decision. He would travel to Verona. Surely a lord like this would appreciate a man of his talent.

He packed carefully. He brought his two best servants, three good horses, and his finest clothes. He arrived in Verona with great hopes.

---

### Part Two: The Waiting

Bergamino performed for Can Grande at dinner one evening. The lord laughed loudly and applauded with genuine pleasure. "Excellent!" he said. "You are very talented." Bergamino smiled and felt hopeful.

But then the days passed. No gift came. No message came.

One week became two. Then three. The inn where Bergamino was staying was not cheap. The food for his servants cost money. The horses needed to be fed. His money was disappearing quickly.

The innkeeper began to look at him with worried eyes. Then, one morning, the innkeeper knocked on the door. "I am sorry, signore," he said quietly. "Your bill is very large. I cannot continue without payment."

Bergamino had almost nothing left. He gave the innkeeper his best suit of clothes — the most beautiful ones, the ones he wore for special performances. The innkeeper took them, nodded, and left.

Bergamino sat on the edge of his bed and stared at the floor.

---

### Part Three: An Unexpected Conversation

The next day, Bergamino went to the palace. He found Can Grande walking in the courtyard. The lord greeted him warmly.

"Bergamino! Good morning. You look a little tired today. Is something wrong?"

Bergamino bowed. "My lord, may I tell you a story?"

Can Grande sat down on a stone bench. "Please."

Bergamino took a breath and began.

---

### Part Four: The Story of Primas and the Bishop

"There was once a great poet and scholar named **Primas**. He was the cleverest man of his generation. Learned men across Europe admired and respected him.

"One day, Primas heard about a wonderful bishop. This bishop was famous for his extraordinary generosity. His table was always full. No one who came to his door left without a gift or a meal. His name was like music in the ears of poor men and travellers.

"Primas thought: 'I will go to see this bishop. Surely he will appreciate a man of my learning. He will give me gifts worthy of my reputation.'

"Primas made the long journey. He arrived at the bishop's house, hungry and tired. He sat at the bishop's table with great hopes.

"But that day — for the very first time in years — the bishop felt something strange come over him. He looked at Primas and felt nothing. No impulse to give. No desire to welcome. He thought, 'Who is this man? Why should I give him anything?'

"And so he gave Primas nothing. Not a cup of soup. Not a piece of bread. Not a single kind word.

"Primas sat at the table for a while. Then he stood up and left. He made the long journey home, hungry and with empty hands."

Bergamino stopped. He looked at Can Grande quietly.

"My lord," he said, in a gentle voice, "in this story, the bishop is you. And I am Primas. I have come a long way. I have waited for many weeks. My best clothes are now with the innkeeper, because I could not pay my bill. My horses are growing expensive. And still, I have received nothing."

---

### Part Five: The Real Can Grande

Can Grande looked at Bergamino for a long moment.

Then he smiled. A real, wide, warm smile.

"You have come a long way," he said. "You have waited patiently and without complaint. And instead of shouting at me or writing me an angry letter, you have told me a story that shows me exactly what I have done — but makes me want to laugh rather than feel ashamed."

He stood up and called his steward.

"Pay every coin of Bergamino's debt at the inn. Bring back his best suit of clothes — whatever the innkeeper paid for them, pay double. And bring three gifts from my own treasury."

He turned to Bergamino. "And you will stay here as my guest, for as long as you like. Your room, your meals, your horses — all paid by my house."

Bergamino bowed very deeply. "Thank you, my lord."

That evening, Bergamino performed for Can Grande and his court. It was said to be one of the finest evenings the palace had ever seen.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **generosity** *(noun)* the quality of giving money, time, or help to others freely and without expecting anything back.
2. **applaud** *(verb)* to clap your hands together to show that you enjoyed a performance or agree with something.
3. **impulse** *(noun)* a sudden, strong feeling that makes you want to do something immediately, without thinking about it first.
4. **reputation** *(noun)* the general opinion that other people have about someone, based on what they have done or how they behave.
5. **innkeeper** *(noun)* the owner of an inn — a small hotel or guest house, especially in older times.
6. **scholar** *(noun)* a person who has studied a subject deeply and knows a great deal about it.
7. **steward** *(noun)* a person who manages the practical affairs of a large house or estate, such as money, food, and staff.
8. **complaint** *(noun)* something you say when you are unhappy about a situation and want it to change; to complain is to express this unhappiness.
9. **treasury** *(noun)* the place where valuable objects or money are kept and stored safely.
10. **generation** *(noun)* all the people who are born and live at about the same time; your parents are one generation, you are the next.
