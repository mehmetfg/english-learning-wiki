# Decameron
## Day 1, Story 8: Ermino and the Painting of Generosity
### A2–B1 Version · Faithful Retelling
*Anlatan: Lauretta*

---

## 📘 Türkçe Özet

**Ermino de' Grimaldi**, Cenova'nın en zengin adamıdır. Gemileri, mülkleri, depoları ve altını vardır. Ama aynı zamanda şehrin en cimri insanıdır. Kendi için bile harcamaz. Eski elbiseler giyer. Basit yemekler yer. Hiçbir zaman yoksullara vermez, hiçbir zaman insanları davet etmez. "Cimri Ermino" diye çağırırlar onu arkasından.

Bir gün Ermino güzel bir ev inşa ettirir. Bu, hayatında yaptığı tek büyük harcamadır. Ev muhteşemdir. İçine duvar resimleri yaptırmak ister. Bir ressam konuşur, başka bir ressam konuşur ama hiçbiri onu tatmin etmez.

O sıralar Genova'ya **Guglielmo Borsiere** adlı zeki ve keskin dilli biri gelir. Ermino onu karşılar ve evi gösterir.

"Söyle bana," der Ermino, "dünyanın en nadir gördüğü, en az bulunan şeyi tabloya dökmemi istiyorum. Ne önerin var?"

Guglielmo düşünür. Duvarları bakar. Ermino'ya bakar. Sonra şöyle der:

"Efendim, en nadir şeyi biliyorum. Bunu duvarınıza çizin: **Cömertlik**."

Uzun bir sessizlik.

Ermino yüzü kızarmış bir şekilde bakar. Kimse daha önce bunu yüzüne söylememişti. İlk kez utandığını hisseder.

Ve o günden sonra Ermino de' Grimaldi değişir. Cimri değil, cömert biri olur.

**Hikâyenin özü:** Doğru söz, doğru anda söylenince, yılların katılığını yumuşatabilir. Guglielmo savaşmadı. Ders vermedi. Sadece tek bir kelime söyledi.

## 🇬🇧 The Story in English (A2–B1)

### Part One: The Richest Miser in Genoa

In the great city of Genoa, there was a man named **Ermino de' Grimaldi**. He was the richest man in the city. Perhaps the richest man in all of Italy at that time. He had ships on the sea, warehouses in the port, land in the country, and gold in many strong boxes.

But Ermino had one enormous fault. He was extraordinarily **stingy**.

He could not spend money. Even for himself. He ate simple food — bread and vegetables, rarely meat. He wore old clothes, even though he could buy the finest. He never invited people to dinner. He never gave gifts. He never gave anything to the poor. Every coin that left his hand caused him almost physical pain.

People in Genoa called him "Ermino the Miser" when he was not listening. Sailors mocked him. Merchants shook their heads. Even his own employees felt no loyalty to him, because he paid as little as possible.

He was rich, but he was not respected.

---

### Part Two: The Beautiful House

One year, Ermino decided to build a new house. He spent more money on it than he had ever spent on anything before. This was his one great luxury. He wanted one beautiful thing in his life.

And the house was indeed beautiful. Large, well-designed, with fine stone floors and high ceilings and good windows that let in the winter light.

He wanted to decorate the walls of the main hall with paintings. He invited painters and asked for their ideas. He listened to many suggestions. But nothing satisfied him.

Around this time, a man visited Genoa — a witty and clever Genoese who had been travelling for years. His name was **Guglielmo Borsiere**. He was known for his sharp mind and his direct tongue. He had a gift for saying the true thing in an interesting way.

Guglielmo came to see the new house. Ermino welcomed him — he always enjoyed free advice. He showed Guglielmo all the rooms, and finally brought him to the beautiful empty main hall.

---

### Part Three: The Question

Ermino stood in the hall and looked at his visitor. "Tell me, Guglielmo," he said. "You are a travelled and intelligent man. You have seen many things. I want to put something remarkable on these walls. Something that almost nobody has ever seen or experienced in real life. Something truly rare. What do you suggest?"

Guglielmo looked at the walls. They were indeed beautiful — smooth white plaster, ready for paint.

He thought for a moment.

Then he looked at Ermino — at his old jacket, at his thin hands, at his careful eyes.

"My dear Ermino," he said, "I know the perfect subject for your wall. Something truly rare. Something that almost nobody sees in their lifetime. Something that would surprise every guest who walks into this room."

"What is it?" Ermino asked eagerly.

"**Generosity**," said Guglielmo.

---

### Part Four: A Long Silence

The room was quiet for a long time.

Ermino looked at Guglielmo. He stood very still. No one had ever said anything like this to his face before. People laughed behind his back. But no one had ever walked into his house and said the true thing so simply and so directly.

He looked at the beautiful empty walls. He thought about his life. His ships, his gold, his old jacket, his plain bread. His empty dinner table.

"No one has ever spoken to me like that before," he said finally.

"I am sorry if it was too direct," said Guglielmo, without any particular apology in his voice.

"No," said Ermino slowly. "You are not wrong. I have great wealth. And I keep it all for myself. This is perhaps not a good way to live."

---

### Part Five: A New Man

Something changed in Ermino that day. Perhaps the right person had said the right thing at exactly the right moment. Perhaps he had been ready to hear it for a long time.

From that week, Ermino de' Grimaldi became a different man. He began to give to the poor. He invited people to his table. He paid his workers more. He helped travellers who came to his door. He gave gifts on special occasions.

People in Genoa were astonished. "What happened to Ermino?" they asked each other. Some said it was a dream. Some said it was illness that had changed him. A few knew the truth.

No one called him "Ermino the Miser" anymore.

And on the wall of his main hall, he had a painting made of a tall, graceful woman with open hands, giving gifts freely to all who came near her.

He called it **Generosity**.

Every morning, when he walked through the hall, he looked at it.

## 📒 Mini Dictionary (English–English, 10 Words)

1. **miser** *(noun)* a person who has a lot of money but refuses to spend it, even when they could easily afford to.
2. **stingy** *(adjective)* not willing to spend money or give things to others; the informal word for a miser's quality.
3. **fault** *(noun)* a bad quality or weakness in a person's character; everyone has faults, but some are more serious than others.
4. **luxury** *(noun)* something expensive and pleasant that you enjoy but do not really need to survive.
5. **plaster** *(noun)* a smooth white material put on walls and ceilings to make them flat and ready for painting.
6. **remarkable** *(adjective)* unusual or impressive in a way that makes people notice and remember it.
7. **astonished** *(adjective)* very surprised — more surprised than you would normally expect to be.
8. **graceful** *(adjective)* moving or looking in a smooth, elegant, and beautiful way.
9. **occasions** *(noun)* special times or events, such as birthdays, festivals, or important moments.
10. **merchant** *(noun)* a person who buys and sells goods to make money, especially in large amounts.
