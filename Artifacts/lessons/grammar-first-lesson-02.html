Mert's Sunday on the South Bank

A quiet day after the airport · ~500 words · 8 grammar structures · daily-life · Mert London Series, Ep. 3

🔊The next morning, while Mert was making breakfast, his mother was reading the news at the kitchen table. They had been planning a quiet Sunday for weeks — no airports, no rush. "If the weather stays nice, we'll walk along the South Bank," Mert said.

🔊His mother smiled. "When I was young, I used to dream of London. I would imagine the bridges, the boats, the small bookshops by the river." She had only a few hours of sleep, but she looked alert and curious.

🔊They left after eleven. The bus that they wanted was already pulling away. Mert tried to wave at the driver, but it was no use. "We've been waiting only two minutes and we've missed it already," he laughed.

🔊"Don't worry," she said. A woman who was standing next to them smiled politely. "If you take the 381, it'll be faster," she suggested. Mert thanked her. There were several buses on this route, and the next one came within minutes.

🔊The South Bank was busy. Street performers, food stalls, some tourists with cameras, a few locals reading on benches. Mert enjoyed watching the crowds. His mother stopped to read a small plaque on a building that had once been a power station — now the Tate Modern.

🔊"I used to think art galleries were boring," she admitted. "But this place is different." Inside, they spent a little time in the photography hall. There were many portraits, and a guide who was leading a small tour said, "If you have time, the top-floor view is worth seeing."

🔊They decided to try it. The lift was crowded, but on the sixth floor, the city opened up — the river, the dome of St Paul's, the small boats moving slowly. His mother wanted to take a picture, but her phone was almost dead. "Mert, the woman who I met yesterday said the wifi here is free. Could you check?"

🔊They had been walking for nearly three hours when hunger arrived. A small cafe that smelled of cinnamon pulled them in. "Do you remember what the woman at the airport said?" his mother asked. "She told me I should try the carrot cake." Mert agreed to share a slice.

🔊Later, on the bus home, his mother said that she had never felt more at ease in a foreign city. "When you were a child," she added, "I would tell you these stories — about the river that runs through London, the people who never sleep. Now I'm seeing it with my own eyes."

🔊Mert smiled. "If we keep walking like this, you'll know London better than I do." Outside, a few lights came on early. Sunday was quietly ending, and tomorrow, his mother said, they should plan to visit Camden.
