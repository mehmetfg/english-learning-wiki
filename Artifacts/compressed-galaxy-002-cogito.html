The Man Who Burned Everything to Find One Truth

Philosophy · A2-B1 · ~470 words · 8 grammar structures · Click any coloured phrase

What if everything you believe is wrong? What if your memories are false? What if the world around you does not really exist? Most people hear these questions and move on. But one man, about four hundred years ago, decided to take them seriously. Very seriously.

His name was René Descartes. And what he did changed the way humans think about thinking.

Descartes was a French philosopher. He lived in the 1600s. At that time, many people accepted old ideas without question. The church said something. The old books said something. And people believed it.

Descartes was not happy with this. He wanted to find things that were truly certain. Not probably true. Not almost true. Certain. So he decided to do something unusual. He decided to doubt everything he had ever thought was true.

He started with his senses — what he could see, hear, and touch. Can you trust your eyes? Not always. You can see things that are not there. You can dream and think it is real.

He said that he could not be certain about anything he saw or felt. So he threw away his senses.

Then he went further. He imagined a very powerful evil spirit — a demon — whose only job was to trick him. Even if a demon was tricking him, was there anything he could know for certain?

He kept thinking. He threw away science. He threw away history. He threw away everything he had ever learned.

And then he stopped. Because he found something he could not throw away.

Here is what Descartes realised. Even if a demon is tricking him, something still has to exist to be tricked. He is thinking right now. He is doubting right now. And you cannot think without existing. You cannot doubt without being real.

He realised that he could not doubt that he was doubting. That is impossible. It is a contradiction.

So he wrote one of the most famous sentences in all of philosophy:

"I think, therefore I am." — Cogito, ergo sum.

Everything else might be false. The world might be an illusion. But one thing is certain: he exists. Because he is thinking.

You do not have to be a philosopher to find this useful. Descartes showed us something important. When everything feels uncertain, when you do not know what to believe, there is always a starting point. You are here. You are thinking. That is already something.

And there is another lesson. Descartes did not accept things just because other people said they were true. He questioned everything himself. That takes courage. Most of us prefer to believe what is comfortable. But sometimes the most interesting truths are hiding behind the questions nobody wants to ask.

What do you believe right now that you have never actually questioned?

✍️ Writing Craft: How to Write About Big Ideas

Bu makalede kullanılan profesyonel felsefe-popüler yazarlığı teknikleri

Anaphora

"What if... What if... What if..." Aynı kelimeyle başlayan ardışık cümleler ritim yaratır.

"What if everything you believe is wrong? What if your memories are false?"

Concrete Verb for Abstract Idea

"Throwing away" senseleri, bilimi — soyut "şüphe etmek" yerine somut "atmak" fiili.

"He threw away his senses. He threw away science. He threw away history."

Famous Quote in Context

"Cogito, ergo sum" çıplak verilmez. Önce yolculuk kurulur, sonra kotasyon doğal olarak gelir.

"So he wrote one of the most famous sentences: 'I think, therefore I am.'"

Closing Question

Yazı soruyla biter. Cevap yok — okuyucunun zihninde devam eder.

"What do you believe right now that you have never actually questioned?"

🧠 Philosophy Connection

Descartes'in radikal şüphesi başka düşünürlerle nasıl konuşur?
Platon — Mağara Alegorisi
Mağarada zincirli, duvarda gölgeleri gerçek sananlar. Descartes'in "duyulara güvensizlik" fikrinin atası: gördüğümüz, gerçek olmayabilir.
💭 "You can dream and think it is real." — Platon 2000 yıl önce aynı şüpheyi başka dille söylemişti.
Aristoteles — First Principles
Her bilgi daha temel bir bilgiye dayanır. En temele inersen, üzerine her şey inşa edilir. Descartes'in "cogito"su modern felsefedeki ilk ilkedir.
💭 Modern tech founders bile "first principles thinking"den bahsederken bu mirası sürdürüyor.
David Hume — Radical Skepticism
Descartes'ten 100 yıl sonra geldi: "Sen kim olduğundan emin misin?" Hume için "ben" denen şey bile bir illüzyondur — sürekli değişen duygu ve düşünce akışı.
💭 Descartes "I am" derken bir "ben" varsayıyor. Ama "ben" gerçekten var mı, yoksa sadece düşünme süreci mi var?

One Thing I Have Never Questioned

Sorgulamadan kabul ettiğin bir inanç/fikir hakkında 5-8 cümle yaz. Aile, okul, başarı, arkadaşlık, din — ne olursa olsun. Sonra sor: bu gerçekten doğru mu?
  • Past Simple: "When I was a child, I believed..."
  • "What if...": "What if this is not true?"
  • Reported Speech: "My parents said that..."
  • Ger/Inf: "I started thinking..." / "I decided to question..."
  • "Even if...": "Even if it is true, I want to know why."
